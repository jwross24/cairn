## Abstract

Let p(n) be the product of the decimal digits of n. Sloane's conjecture states that every integer reaches a single
digit after at most 11 iterations of p. Brier, Clavier, Gutsche and Naccache (2021) settled the case of odd digital
roots by enumerating the antecedent trees of 1, 3, 7, 9 (and bounding the root-5 case); the even nonzero roots 2, 4,
6, 8 were left open, and Fonga's 2-adic exponent bound (arXiv:2608.27802, 2026) later made each vertex of the four
even trees a finite problem. We report
a computer-assisted enumeration of these trees. The trees T_2, T_4, T_6, T_8 built from the 8,807 zero-free
{2,3,7}-units below 10^4000 have 177 vertices (roots included). 143 of them are enumerated to level 96, and for the
138 that went through the final stage we establish that the children are exactly the known ones: 51 by a
residue-pattern enumeration that leaves no open pattern, 45 by the same enumeration followed by a 10-adic cut (Lemma C
at depth 1) on the 8,027 patterns that remain open, and 42 by a "sandwich" between Lemma C at depth 1–2 and an explicit
two-ended Baker bound (Theorem U″: Matveev top-down, Yu's 2007 Theorem II bottom-up). The 5 vertices enumerated after
the final-stage snapshot are closed below 1.3×10^27 digits, and 34 vertices are not enumerated; their level-12 pattern
counts are between 1.1×10^12 and 1.4×10^22, with 13 vertices (Tier B, k_max 20–29) and 6 vertices (Tier C, k_max
31–44, 2.9×10^22 patterns in all) beyond any enumeration we can run. In T_4 the seven small vertices are closed, so
every integer with digital root 4 and persistence at least 4 passes through the vertex 1161216. Every open pattern
met in the enumeration is anchored on a small {2,3,7}-unit (12,190 recorded patterns, 388 anchors), but we show this
is not a theorem: residue survival is uniform at every level we can test, so the open set of a Tier-C vertex is
predicted to contain accidental, unanchored patterns, and anchor-first enumeration is a forecast, not a certificate.
In base 8 the analogous trees (70 vertices) are enumerated to level 96 on all 70 vertices and closed outright on 57 so
far (the sandwich is still running), and in base 6 (29 vertices) on 17. The frontier is stated as a problem: an
effective per-gap bound with exponent θ < 1 in the height of the lower part would close Tier C at depth 1; every known
theorem has θ = 1.

Conditionally on the Robert–Stewart–Tenenbaum refined abc conjecture with constant C₁ = 1 (an explicit hypothesis),
every vertex with at most 31 non-1 digits is closed by the existing enumeration below 10^4000 alone, so the trees of
digital roots 2 and 4 are exactly as computed and the persistence of integers with digital root 4 is at most 5; the
remaining five vertices close once the enumeration reaches 10^6883.

## 1. Introduction

**1.1 Sloane's conjecture.** For a positive integer n let p(n) be the product of its decimal digits. Iterating p
reaches a single digit, the multiplicative digital root Δ(n); the number of steps is the persistence of n. Sloane
(1973) conjectured that the persistence in base 10 is at most 11; 277777788888899 attains 11, and no larger value is
known. Since p(n) is a product of digits, every iterate after the first is of the form 2^a 3^b 5^c 7^d, and if a digit
0 or a digit 5 together with an even digit occurs, the orbit falls to 0; if 5 occurs with only odd digits the orbit
stays on multiples of 5 and ends in 0 or 5. Hence for Δ(n) ∉ {0, 5} every iterate p^j(n), 1 ≤ j < pers(n), is a
zero-free {2,3,7}-unit y ≥ 10 ("zero-free S-unit"), and the orbits are organized by the *antecedent trees*: the
vertices of A_Δ are the zero-free S-units whose orbit ends at Δ, with an edge y → p(y).

**1.2 The odd case (BCGN 2021).** Brier, Clavier, Gutsche and Naccache proved the conjecture for odd Δ by showing that
A_1, A_3, A_7, A_9 are finite and enumerating them: for each vertex s they determine all y ∈ S with p(y) = s (the
*children* of s) by a modular lifting argument. They listed Δ ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8} as open [BCGN; cited through RESULT.md
and PAPER.md].

**1.3 The even case and the 2-adic bound.** For even Δ a child y of s may be divisible by a high power of 2, and the
exponent a = v_2(y) is not bounded by the digit multiset alone in any obvious way. Fonga (arXiv:2608.27802) bounds a
for each vertex (the bound is decided by the last few digits of y: enumerate the tails over the digit
alphabet divisible by 2^k until none exist, RESULT.md step 1), which makes the children of each vertex a finite
problem; the paper did not perform the search. Its size was estimated beyond classical reach. (HYPOTHESES.md
records that Fonga's Theorem 4.1 gives the exact maximal v_2 per digit multiset by a finite recursion; the arXiv
abstract, fetched 2026-10-21, confirms it is the paper that resolves BCGN's conjecture on the bounded exponent of 2
in the families with fixed digit multiplicities, in every base b ≥ 3 and for every prime p | b.)

**1.4 What is new here.** (i) An enumeration engine for the children of a vertex, built on residue patterns modulo
the primes of 10^L − 1 at levels L = 12, 24, 48, 96, with exact lattice cosets in the exponents (b, c), 10-adic
pinning and a 2-adic descent (§3.1–3.4). (ii) The observation that every pattern that survives level 96 in our
runs is an exact improper representation of a small S-unit (an *anchor*, §3.5), which explains why no modular method
can close it, and a proof that this is not a theorem (§6.2). (iii) Lemma C, the 10-adic cut at the full period
(§3.6), which closes most anchored patterns outright, and the sandwich (§3.7), which closes the rest for k ≤ 11 by
comparing a lattice lower bound with an explicit two-ended Baker upper bound (Theorems U, U′, U″). (iv) The results:
Theorems 1–3 (§2), the per-vertex table `TABLES.md` Table A, the anchor table (Table B), the frontier with its exact
costs (Table D), and partial results in bases 8 and 6 (Table C). (v) A statement of the frontier as a problem about
per-gap bounds (§6).

## 3. Method

**3.1 Residue patterns.** For a prime p ∤ 10, y mod p depends only on the residues of n and the e_j modulo
ord_p(10). At level L the primes used are those of 10^L − 1 outside {2, 3, 5, 7}: at level 12 the five primes 11,
13, 37, 101, 9901; level 24 adds 73, 137, 99990001; level 48 adds 17, 5882353, 9999999900000001; level 96 adds 97,
353, 449, 641, 1409, 69857, 206209, 66554101249, 75118313082913 (RESULT.md step 2). A *pattern* at level L is
(n mod L, multiset of pairs (d, e mod L)); nothing in the method sees the order of equal digits, so the multiset of
pairs is the correct unit (RESULT.md addendum). The condition y ≡ 2^a 3^b 7^c (mod p) is, after discrete logarithms,
linear in (a, b, c) modulo p − 1; the system over the primes of a level is solvable if and only if the vector of
logarithms lies in the image of Z^3, a condition that decomposes into ℓ-primary components and is tested by table
lookup (the prefilter), and its solution set is a coset of a fixed lattice Λ_L in (b, c), assembled by CRT. At level
12 the subgroup <2, 3, 7> has index 40 in (Z/P)^*, P = 11·13·37·101·9901, and about 19.2 % of pattern values are
zero divisors, so the generic survival rate is (1 − 0.192)/40 = 1/49.5 (RESULT-dpcount.md §1.1, §4; the "1 in 40" of
PAPER.md §2.2 and the "1/600" of RESULT.md count different stages, NOTE-2026-10-21 §8 item 7).

**3.2 Levels, lifting and cosets.** A pattern at level L has at most 2^{k+1} children at level 2L (fewer after the
multiset symmetry); the new primes' congruences are imposed on the inherited coset. The coset arithmetic is exact
(Lagrange-reduced bases, unit-tested against brute force on 300 random systems, RESULT.md). The lattice Λ_96 of the
20 primes of level 96 has index 2.810×10^56 in Z^2 and shortest vector of length 7.850×10^27 (DEEPCUT.md; reproduced
by the blind re-implementation).

**3.3 10-adic pinning and descent.** On a coset, 2^a 3^b 7^c mod 10^K is constant for K up to K*(level, a), which is
3, 4, 5, 5 at levels 12, 24, 48, 96 (RESULT-accidents.md Table 1); the lowest K* digits of a candidate are therefore
known and must be digits of the pattern, or the number itself when n ≤ K*. If t = v_2(9Y − 10^{K*}) differs from a,
the next non-1 digit or the end of the number lies in a bounded window, which is enumerated; each case extends the
known tail, cuts the coset by three linear congruences (one modulo 4·5^{K−1}, a parity, one modulo 2^{K−a−2}) and
recurses (Lemma B, RESULT.md step 6). The blind audit observed that the descent is silent whenever a < K*, so
closure always comes from the tail test, an empty coset or an exact candidate (RESULT.md, Audit item 1).

**3.4 Outcomes and the lattice bound.** A pattern is dead, exact (y determined and verified) or open. An equation is
closed when no pattern is open, a vertex when all its equations are. For an open pattern at level 96 the coset's
shortest vector gives: all but at most one coset point have max(b, c) ≥ 2.8×10^27, hence at least 1.3×10^27 digits;
the exceptional point is computed by Babai rounding and checked (`digit_bound` in the sweep files; RESULT.md T0′).

**3.5 Anchors (carry coincidences).** For a level-96 pattern with value V = R_ν + Σ (d_j − 1)10^{e_j} (positions read
as integers in [0, 96)), an *anchor* is an S-unit y with 0 < y < R_96 and y ≡ V (mod R_96), equivalently y + kR_96 = V
for some integer κ with 0 ≤ κ < 1 + 8k (OPEN-PATTERNS.md (†); NOTE-2026-10-21 Prop. 1.1). An anchored pattern is open
at every level: every candidate y′ of the pattern satisfies y′ ≡ y (mod R_96), so no prime of any level separates y′
from y (Lean `candidate_modEq`). The anchors are either the vertex's own child (SELF: a = t silences Lemma B) or a
number from a sibling tree impersonated by digits whose (d − 1) contributions carry — e.g. 111132 = 2^2·3^4·7^3 read
with a 2 above the top as a sixth leading 1 (OPEN-PATTERNS.md). Over the 143 enumerated vertices the sweep files
record 12,190 distinct (vertex, M, a, y) open triples on 388 distinct anchors, 54 of them SELF; 106 anchors contain
a digit 0 (genuine S-units the residues cannot tell from zero-free ones); none fails to be a {2,3,7}-unit; 124 exceed
10^9 and 77 exceed 10^10; the largest is 100461232161612 = 2^2·3^21·7^4 (Table B). 111132 anchors 679 patterns in 66
vertices, 13122 = 2·3^8 anchors 449 in 51, and 330812181 = 3^9·7^5 anchors 318 in 32 (Table B).

**3.6 Lemma C (the deep cut).** Let P be an open level-96 pattern with 2-adic exponent a, positions e_j ∈ [0, 96),
and coset C in (b, c), and suppose every point of C other than one checked point gives a candidate with at least 192
digits (true by §3.4). Then every other candidate y′ satisfies, at depth D,

    y′ ≡ R_{96D} + Σ_j (d_j − 1)·10^{e_j + 96 t_j}   (mod 10^{96D}),   t_j ∈ {0, …, D − 1} or "≥ D",

with distinct positions, so v_2(L) = a and 3^b 7^c ≡ L/2^a modulo 5^{96D} and modulo 2^{96D−a}: three linear
congruences in (b, c) (`descent.cut_coset`; the arithmetic core is the Lean lemma `deep_cut_core`). If for every
admissible choice of the t_j the congruences are inconsistent with C, P is closed apart from the checked point
(DEEPCUT.md). What makes the cut bite is the index of Λ_96: the image of Λ_96 in (Z/5^96)^* × (Z/2^{96−a})^* is the
kernel of reduction to (Z/5^5)^* × (Z/2^9)^*, of index 4·5^4·2^8 = 640,000 (DEEPCUT.md blind audit; NOTE-2026-10-21
Prop. 3.2), so a depth-1 case survives with probability about 1.6×10^−6 and the verdict depends only on the pattern's
digits in the classes e < a + 9. Depth 2 never kills (the depth-1 sublattice maps onto the kernel of G_192 → G_96);
deeper cuts only raise the bound, by about 10^48 digits per depth. On the 05:45 sweep snapshot Lemma C at depth 1
closes 637 of 680 anchored patterns (93.7 %, DEEPCUT-run-final-d1.txt); over the 138 vertices of the final stage it
closes 6,692 of 8,027 (83.4 %), the rate falling with k (TABLES.md, source notes).

**3.7 The sandwich and Theorem U.** *Upper bound (Theorem U, BAKER-CHAIN.md).* If y′ = 2^a 3^b 7^c has the form (E)
with k non-1 digits, then n < G(k, a), where G is obtained by applying Matveev's lower bound for linear forms in
logarithms (BMS Thm 9.4, C(t) = 1.4·30^{t+3} t^{4.5}) once per digit gap, the part of the number above the gap
entering the next form as a rational of height ≤ (n − E_j) log 10 + log 9, and solving the resulting self-referential
inequality n ≤ Φ_{k,a}(n) by a monotonicity lemma (Φ(ν)/ν decreases on [e^k, ∞)). Numerically log10 G(k, 0) ≈
11.49 + 13.89k and log10 G(k, a ≥ 1) ≈ 13.37 + 15.64k plus log-log terms: log10 G = 111.704 (k = 6, a = 0), 128.488
(7, 0), 145.328 (8, 0), 161.654 (8, 1), 196.1 (11, 0), 598.7 (31, a ≥ 1), 849.7 (44, a ≥ 1) (BAKER-CHAIN.md §6;
Table D). The bound depends on a only through the number of logarithms (3 for a = 0, 4 for a ≥ 1) and a "+a" inside a
logarithm. *Lower bound.* The sub-cosets left by Lemma C at depth D have exact nearest points (Babai rounding with a
±2 window, proved exact for a Lagrange-reduced basis, REDTEAM-SANDWICH.md §6), and every candidate has at least
‖p_nearest‖/√2 · log10 3 digits: about 10^72 at D = 1, 10^120 at D = 2, 10^168 at D = 3, 10^216 at D = 4. *Sandwich.*
If the lower bound at some depth exceeds G(k, a), the pattern has no candidate. On the 43 patterns of
SANDWICH-full.txt the verdict under Theorem U is CLOSED at depths 2, 3, 4 (7, 26, 10 patterns) with margins between
10^4.96 and 10^43.48 digits; e.g. s = 14336, M = 47888, a = 0, anchor 7411887, k = 5: G = 10^94.98, depth 2, 2
sub-cosets, lower bound 10^120.07. Under Theorem U″ (§3.9) the same pattern has G″ = 10^45.34 and closes at depth 1
with lower bound 10^72.26, and the 43 close at depths 1 and 2 (33 and 10) with 792 sub-cosets in all
(SANDWICH-full-U2.txt). The cost of the depth-D enumeration is about (D + 1)^k sub-cosets (every extension of a depth-1
survivor survives, SANDWICH.md "Cost correction"; NOTE-2026-10-21 Prop. 5.3), 59,124 in all on the 43 patterns;
identical digits are counted as distinguishable by the C port, so the number of distinct sub-cosets is only 3,902
(RESULT-peranchor.md §0).

**3.8 Certifying the lower bound without enumerating the last blocks (box-constrained CVP).** RESULT-peranchor.md
replaces the enumeration of the copy choices in the last two blocks by one lattice computation per start node. Fix a
depth-1 (or depth-2) survivor node with suffix known modulo 10^s. For a coset whose units agree modulo 10^s and
1 ≤ h ≤ s − a, the unit at v_0 + z_1 u_1 + z_2 u_2 is ≡ y_0 + z_1 y_0 t_1 + z_2 y_0 t_2 (mod 10^{s+h}), t_i = 3^{u_{i,1}}
7^{u_{i,2}} − 1 (the linearization of NOTE-2026-10-21 §5.1; Lean `two_generator_lift` covers the core). Hence the
union U over all admissible placements x ∈ {0,1}^f of the remaining digits in positions [s, s + h) of the cut
cosets is {v_0 + z_1 u_1 + z_2 u_2 : A·z − W·x ≡ β (mod 10^h), x admissible}, a coset of a lattice in Z^{2+f}
intersected with box and at-most-one-per-group constraints. Embedding with penalty weight λ = ⌊N⌋ + 1 turns
"min_U ‖(b, c)‖ > N" into "no lattice point within squared radius 4N^2 + λ^2(f + G) of a target", which integral LLL
(δ = 0.99, in dimension 2 + f ≤ 16 on the 43 patterns) followed by exact Schnorr–Euchner enumeration certifies with 0
enumeration nodes on all 263 start nodes; the exact minima agree with the sub-coset enumeration on 88/88
representations and with the C port on 43/43, and 54/54 planted union points are found. The enumeration of the
words below the start s remains: the exponent of the cost is roughly halved, not removed. The parameter that
decides the cost is r, the number of digits the depth-1 survivor leaves unplaced (r ≤ 7 on all 43 patterns); it has
not been measured on Tier-A/B survivors.

**3.9 The two-ended chains: Theorem U′ (B3) and Theorem U″ (Yu 2007).** Reading each M·T_j^{−1} − 1 5-adically (T_j
the part of 9y′ + 1 below position E_j; 5 ∤ T_j when the digit 5 is excluded, and a ≥ 1 forces E_k = 0) gives a
bottom-up chain, and meeting it with the top-down chain at the best index j bounds n ≤ min_j (X_j + Z_j). With the
Bugeaud–Kaneko constant (arXiv:1704.00432, Thm 2.1, (2.2)) on the 5-adic side this is Theorem U′: log10 G′ = 281.5 /
279.2 / 280.1 at k = 31 (a = 0 / 1 / 40) and 402.1 / 393.9 / 394.8 at k = 44 (B3-REPORT.md), PROVEN-ON-PAPER with
interval-certified numerics but conditional on a constant no lane audited against Yu's original, and with the fold of
2^a certified for a ≤ 40 only. Theorem U″ replaces the 5-adic step by Yu, Forum Math. 19 (2007), Theorem II (proved in
print), applied over K = Q(i) at the prime (2 + i) so that hypothesis (1.5) holds (ζ_4 ∈ K), with n = 3 logarithms
(3, 7, 2^a/T_j), and with the multiplicatively dependent case T_j = ±2^a 3^u 7^v handled by Yu's Theorem 1; the chain
takes the maximum of the two branches at every stage (NOTES-u2.md). Its certified table utable_U2.txt covers k ≤ 24,
a ≤ 24 (600 entries, each ≤ the Theorem U value, certified by 256-bit interval arithmetic; second typing by a
fixed-point iteration agrees to 1.2×10^−6; planted negative at 0.99 of the fixed point refused), and NOTES-u2.md gives
log10 G″ = 246.02 / 232.78 / 233.49 at k = 31 and 344.98 / 334.99 / 334.99 at k = 44 (a = 0 / 1 / 40); for k = 8,
11, 22, 24 and a = 1 the table gives 63.7, 80.6, 164.2, 181.1 against 161.7, 217.8, 426.3, 464.5 under Theorem U
(`baker_upper_bound`; Table D). The sandwich depth needed at k = 31 falls from 12 (U) through 6 (U′) to
5 (U″), and at k = 44 from 18 through 8 to 7. Stated gaps (NOTES-u2.md): Yu's Part II prints twelve "we omit the
details" statements, not reconstructed; the Z-side monotonicity lemma that resolves n ≤ Φ(n) for the non-polynomial
5-adic side is a paper argument plus a grid check of 717,931 points with 0 violations, not a certified computation;
Matveev's step and the application identities are reused from Theorem U unchanged; an earlier audit omitted the
factor e^n in Yu's constant (1.7), so the earlier figures 230.2 / 324.3 were understated. Theorem U″ is a candidate
in NOTES-u2.md ("not adopted"); it is the table the final stage of Theorem 2 was run with.

**3.10 The shadow certificate and the content lemma (HSHADOW.md, EXPERIMENTS-X.md §X5, §X8).** Let d₁ | d₂ be the
invariant factors of ⟨3,7⟩ ⊂ (Z/Q_96)^*, so that [Z² : Λ_96] = d₁d₂; the content of Λ_96 (the gcd of all coordinates
of its vectors) is exactly d₁ = 2^7·3^2·5^4·7·11·41·59·101, and the words' sub-lattice Λ′_a = Λ_96 ∩ {3^b7^c ≡ 1 mod
5^96·2^{96−a}} has content 2500·d₁ (a ≤ 85; 2^7-part for a ≥ 87). For a prime p ∉ P_96 with (p − 1) | content, every
vector of Λ′_a maps to 1 in (Z/p)^*, so the cut of a word's coset at p is a single congruence on the child's value:
with V_child = V + (10^{96} − 1)·Δ(S, sn), Δ = Σ_{j∈S}(d_j − 1)10^{e_j} + sn·10^ν/9 (S the unplaced digits moved to odd
copies, sn the ν-shift), the child passes p iff Δ ≡ 0 (mod p). At level 192 this holds for 193 and 769 (image
trivial for every a ≤ 160, verified), hence a level-96 word's children survive 193·769 iff Δ ≡ 0 (mod 148417): the
empty subset — the *shadow*, the anchor's identity continued — always does, and on 256/256 words of 200 patterns and
on all 43 level-96 sandwich survivors no other child does (2433/2433 non-shadow children die at the prime cut, none at
the 10-adic window). The certificate `shadowcert.py` lists the (S, sn) with Δ ≡ 0 by one subset-sum DP over Z/148417
across all k digits of the pattern (expected candidates 2^{k+1}/148417; 0 on the 43), tests any candidate exactly,
and then cuts each word's shadow by the level-384 primes and the K = 384 window; it reproduces the 43 verdicts of the
sandwich and of the full word lift exactly (37 closed with no survivor, 6 with bound 10^{238–239} against U″ ≤
10^{66}). The same lemma lists the primes where the kill is additive at any level: at level 480 (×5) seven PH-feasible
primes (31, 41, 61, 211, 241, 3541, 9091) have trivial image, so the 96 → 480 word lift (`wordlift480.py`, validated
by planted placements 296/297 and the Δ′ identity 500/500) kills every non-shadow child by Δ′ ≡ 0 mod ≈ 1.2×10^{18}
and bounds the survivors at 10^{302} digits at depth 1 — enough for k ≤ 38, and with depth 2 for k = 44. Nothing in
this section depends on the depth enumeration of §3.7–3.8; the final stage's cost per pattern becomes
k·148417 + (number of words)·O(1).

**3.10′ Why the cuts stop: the witness lemma and the compatibility group (HSHADOW.md §8, EXPERIMENTS-X.md §X20).**
For a wrap-0 representation V = y the anchor's own exponent vector (b_y, c_y) satisfies 2^a 3^b 7^c ≡ y modulo *every*
prime, so the shadow's modular coset at every level L is (b_y, c_y) + Λ_L: no prime cut, at any level, kills a wrap-0
shadow (Cairn's 256/256 shadows alive at 192 is this lemma, not luck). What the anchor does not satisfy is the length:
the window 2^a 3^b 7^c ≡ R_K + Σ_fixed (d_j − 1)10^{e_j} (mod 10^K) with K = L defines a second class v_w + K_L,
K_L = {(b, c): 3^b 7^c ≡ 1 (mod 10^L)}, and the shadow survives level L iff the two classes meet, i.e. iff
δ := v_w(L) − (b_y, c_y) ∈ Λ_L + K_L — one lattice membership in the finite {2,5}-group G_L = Z²/(Λ_L + K_L). Computed
exactly (`gtable.py`): |G_96| = |G_192| = 2^9·5^4, |G_384| = |G_768| = 2^13·5^4, |G_480| = 2^10·5^4, |G_288| = |G_576| =
2^9·5^6, |G_672| = 2^9·5^4, |G_960| = |G_1056| = 2^10·5^4, |G_1152| = 2^13·5^6. This is the whole story of the
cut/sandwich split: level 384 is the 2-adic killer (1,153 of 1,335 cut), 480 adds one bit (359 cut, a subset), 288 is
an independent 5-adic killer, and 768 adds nothing (all six sandwich survivors of the 43 alive at 768, bound 10^{441}).
The test reproduces the pipeline's level-384 survivor count on 43/43 patterns (74/74 words) and predicted, pattern for
pattern and before the run, which two of the six a ×3 step 384 → 1152 closes (`extinct1152.py`: both CLOSED-cut, the
other four at 10^{633}; of Cairn's 182 level-384 sandwich patterns 181 are 480-sandwich too, so the 1152 step is the
only cheap route that converts some of them). A shadow surviving every level would be an adelic solution of
9·2^a 3^b 7^c − 10^n = c₀ with c₀ = 9Σ(d_j − 1)10^{e_j} − 1; "the cuts eventually close every pattern" is Skolem's
local–global principle for this three-term exponential equation, open in that generality (Schinzel 1975 and
Bartolome–Bilu–Luca 2013 cover one product term or rank one; Bérczes–Hajdu–Tijdeman cover x^n − bΠy_i^{k_i} = ±1).
Among the PH-feasible primes of levels ≤ 1152 no prime adds more than 2^5 of depth on its own (`align.py`; the depth is
a saturation property of the whole lattice, bounded by the 2,5-parts of |⟨3,7⟩ mod p| and cut by the misalignment of the
modular and q-adic logarithms), so for the robust survivors the sandwich is the needed effective substitute, with margin
≥ 110 orders at 384 and ≥ 560 at 1152.

**3.11 Additive lifts (LIFT-ADDITIVE.md, EXPERIMENTS-X.md §X1, §X3, §X11).** The same mechanism one level down: a
level-12 survivor fixes a coset v_a + Λ_12 (content 900 = d₁(⟨3,7⟩ mod Q_12)); a level-24 child passes a new prime
p ∈ {73, 137, 99990001} iff Δ ≡ (2^a x_p h − V)/(10^{12} − 1) for some h in the image of Λ_12 (orders 2, 17, 22220),
so a DP over Z/(73·137) lists the children passing both small primes (≈ 1/115 of the 2^{q+1}) and only those are
tested exactly (the primes are correlated through the coset, so the per-prime sets are necessary, not sufficient).
Validated child-set-for-child-set against brute force on 120/120 real level-12 survivors; at 24 → 48 (12/12) only
17 is additive (prefilter 1/8); at 48 → 96 (12/12) the DP over 97·353 leaves one candidate per parent (1/2389). A
deeper 10-adic cut before the lift changes none of this (the cut touches only the 2- and 5-parts of the images and
saturates at the first window), and no first level other than 12 reduces the total (exact table for L = 3 … 60):
the number of level-12 parents is the quantity no modulus choice moves.

## 5. Other bases

The method is base-independent: for base b the digit primes are the primes below b; those dividing b have bounded
exponents (a p-adic tail enumeration), the others are free and carry the lattice; the level primes are those of
b^L − 1 outside the digit primes (PAPER.md §5; `engine_b.py`). Theorem U generalizes with (b − 2)b and b − 1 in place
of 80 and 9 and the option of taking only prime logarithms; the certified ceilings at k = 6/8/10/12/16 are 107/140/
172/205/271 (base 6), 123/160/197/235/310 (base 8) and 139/181/222/264/348 (base 12) in log10 (RESULT-base-b.md §5).
Base 8 (lattice rank 3, one base prime) closes less well at depth 1 than base 10 (the 2-adic cut is weakest for a = 0
shadows of the vertex's own children, PROGRAM-BASES.md); the level-384 lift gives ≥ 10^141 digits, above G for k ≤ 9
and at depth 2 for k = 10, which is why 58 base-8 vertices close by the sandwich (Table C). Base 6 (rank 1) closes 17
of 29 vertices by the residue ladder alone. The census of PROGRAM-BASES.md for bases 9 (174 vertices, 108 with ≤
2×10^8 patterns) and 12 (150 vertices, 118 feasible) is a census only; no base-9 or base-12 result file is among the
inputs of this draft. Every open pattern in base 6 is anchored (20 of 20, OPEN-PATTERNS.md); the base-8 open patterns
were not classified in the files at hand ⟨?⟩.

## 6. The frontier as a problem

**6.1 Tiers and costs.** Table D gives the 19 vertices of Tiers B and C with exact pattern counts, k_max, the
Baker ceilings and, for Tier C, the Monte-Carlo survivor counts of RESULT-dpcount.md. At the measured C level-12 rate
of 8 ns per pattern Tier B costs 290–14,508 core-days per vertex and Tier C 1.6×10^6–1.3×10^9 (FRONTIER.md); a
hypothetical 10^10 patterns/s kernel brings Tier B to 4–181 days per vertex and leaves Tier C at 2×10^4–1.6×10^7 days.
The 12 → 24 lift is the actual wall: the level-24 children of the level-12 survivors number 1.0×10^24–9.0×10^27 per
Tier-C vertex even with the exact multiset symmetry (2^{20.9}–2^{25.8} children per survivor instead of 2^{k+1}),
and the engine's 2-adic bound does not terminate for k ≥ 36 (the true A is at least 118–144, RESULT-dpcount.md §1.4).
An exact level-12 survivor count is a 64-GB dense DP over Z/P per equation, about 100 node-days for the 6,251
Tier-C equations, and would only confirm N/49.5 (RESULT-dpcount.md). For Tier B the lift lane measured, on about 330
equations with k = 6–21, that the 12 → 24 child tests per pattern grow ×1.60 per digit and the 24 → 48 lift ×1.67
per digit at 95–230 ns per child, that a hashed join of the second child half against the exactly computed coset at
level 24 (`lvlJ.c`, byte-identical to the plain lift on 97/97 equations) gains ×11–14 at k = 20, and that a dual
level-12 formulation matches production on 4 multisets; on the resulting model (k > 21 extrapolated, A = 40 and a
×3 level-48 join assumed) all 13 Tier-B vertices cost 1.8×10^3 Mac-days with the join against 3.7×10^4 with the
production engine, the six smallest (k = 20–26) 5–17 Mac-days each and the k = 25–29 vertices 400–1,300 core-days
each; none of this is run end to end or adopted into the engine (HYPOTHESES.md, "Tier B lane results").

**6.2 Anchoring is a forecast, not a theorem.** Every open pattern recorded in the base-10 sweep is anchored (Table
B: 12,190 of 12,190 triples carry an S-unit exceptional point; NOT-S-UNIT 0; the exact identity (†) was checked by
`run_exact` on 3,153 of 3,154 triples of an earlier snapshot, H1-REPORT.md §2), and an anchor-first census of the six
Tier-C vertices predicts 1.9×10^11 to 1.5×10^14 anchored patterns per vertex, agreeing within ×0.9–1.7 with the
sweep-calibrated forecast of FRONTIER.md (RESULT-anchorfirst.md Table 2). But the statement "every pattern open at
level 96 is anchored" is false: for u = 3^156·7^214 the pattern read off the digits of u mod R_96 passes all 20
prime congruences, survives the engine's tail test and descent, and has no anchor at any a (the 95-digit value is
not a {2,3,7}-unit) — a construction with q = 100 digits, outside any program vertex (H1-REPORT.md §4;
NOTE-2026-10-21 Prop. 1.5). Whether such *accidents* occur at Tier-C size was tested directly (RESULT-accidents.md):
for uniformly random level-96 patterns of four multisets (q = 11, 11, 13, 14) the residue-survival probability equals
|H_L|/Q_L at level 12 (to 0.1 %), at level 24 (0.996 ± 0.011 pooled, via lifts) and at level 48 (1.12 ± 0.10, 1,275
survivors), and the lifts of anchored parents produce unanchored survivors at exactly the uniform rate (641 observed
against 644 predicted at 12 → 24). The earlier "model overshoots by 29–480×" (H1-REPORT.md §7) was an accounting
error: it used the <2,3,7> index in place of the fixed-a index (a factor 75 at 12 → 24) and omitted the 10-adic tail
test; with both, the uniform model predicts 0.1–0.2 accidents at level 48 for s = 124416, M = 2^9 3^5, and 0 were
observed. Extrapolated to level 96, the uniform model predicts 1.7×10^5–1.6×10^13 residue accidents per Tier-C vertex
before the tail test and about 10^1–10^10 after it, 10^−11–10^−4 of the anchored count (RESULT-accidents.md Table 8).
Each such pattern has a coset whose nearest point is about 10^27 away (no small anchor), a 10-adic tail consistent
to 5 digits by selection, and escapes Lemma C at depth 1 once 2^q ≫ 6.4×10^5. A character-sum bound can prove only
that accidents exist in the predicted number, never their absence. Consequently an anchor-first enumeration of a
Tier-C vertex undercounts its open set, and anchoring cannot be turned into a certificate by excluding accidents.

**6.3 Per-anchor closure.** The hope that one computation per anchor decides all of its patterns was assessed and
killed: as a relaxation the branching is (1 + |M|) per position up to the frontier position m* ≈ 27.8k − 25 where
the lattice index first exceeds the Baker radius (3^184 nodes at k = 11), and as sharing it is empty (263 start nodes
on the 43 patterns, 263 distinct keys, zero reuse even for the anchor 330812181 with 11 patterns) (RESULT-peranchor.md
§5). The right unit is the depth-1 survivor node, and the box-CVP of §3.8 makes it cheap when r is small.

**6.4 The precise target.** Every effective bound for S-units with few nonzero digits has the shape (C log n)^{k+O(1)}
(Stewart 1980; Bugeaud–Kaneko 2018; Bugeaud 2018, 2026; LIT2.md), because Baker/Matveev/Yu give "gap ≤ C·h(rest)·
log B" with C ≳ 10^13, so the admissible size multiplies at every digit; the single-ended chain is geometric by
construction (NOTE-2026-10-21 §6.2 B1) and the two-ended scheme cannot drop below (k/2) log10 λ. A per-gap bound of
the form gap ≤ C·h^θ with S_{j+1} = S_j + C·S_j^θ would give log10 S_k = 17.8 / 34.8 / 67.8 / 158 / 520 at k = 31 for
θ = 0 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 0.9 / 1 (B3-REPORT.md): θ ≤ 0.75 closes Tier C at depth 1, θ = 0 closes it below the lattice's
10^27, and every known theorem has θ = 1. Alternatively Baker constants smaller by about 10^8 (10^−8 at k = 31,
10^−12 at k = 44 for depth 3, measured against Theorem U′) would do; neither exists unconditionally. Theorem U″ moves
the k = 44 ceiling to 10^335, which still needs depth 7, i.e. 8^44 ≈ 10^40 sub-cosets per surviving pattern at Tier C
(or, with the box-CVP of §3.8, the enumeration of the words below the start of the linear range). The Subspace theorem gives finiteness of each
equation ineffectively; an abc-strength input (Robert–Stewart–Tenenbaum) would collapse Tier C to about 5×10^3 digits,
a finite search, but is not admissible as a proof (LIT2.md §3). Every complete resolution of a many-term exponential
equation on record (Dimitrov–Howe 2021: 22–25 terms, ×3.5 per term; Bertók–Hajdu 2016; Bajpai–Bennett 2023: 5 terms)
is a residue-class enumeration paying exponentially per term (LIT2.md).

**6.5 Tier C as a conditional theorem (CONDITIONAL.md).** The exponential shape of §6.4 comes entirely from the
constant C ≫ 1 in "gap ≤ C·h(rest)·log B"; any abc-type hypothesis with exponent 1 + o(1) makes every gap at most
ε·n + C_ε and the chain polynomial in k. Among the conjectures in print, the only ones with a usable shape are not
fully explicit (Masser–Oesterlé's κ(ε), Lang–Waldschmidt's C(ε), the n-conjecture's K(n, ε)); the fully explicit
ones either give nothing (Granville–Tucker c < rad², the IUT-claimed effective abc: exponent ≥ 1 + 1/(k+1) is too
weak) or astronomical numbers (Baker's explicit abc via Chim–Shorey–Sinha: 10^{83}–10^{133} digits, because ω(abc) is
unbounded in our triples). The weakest explicit hypothesis that closes the trees is Robert–Stewart–Tenenbaum's refined
abc (Bull. LMS 46 (2014), Conjecture A) with its one unspecified constant fixed: **RST[C₁]**, c < rad·exp(4√(3 log rad
/ log log rad)·(1 + log log log rad/(2 log log rad) + C₁/log log rad)) for all coprime triples with rad ≥ 16, headline
C₁ = 1 (above their model value β = 0.597; consistent with every known abc triple for any C₁ ≥ 0, a violation needing
merit > 48 against the record 38.67). Applying it to the three triples of each digit gap (rad ≤ 16800·10^n) gives the
master inequality n ≤ (k+1)(4.2253 + Φ(n)/log 10) and, every inequality written out and every value verified at N(k)
and N(k)+1 (`nk.py`):

**Theorem C (conditional, explicit).** Assume RST[1]. A zero-free {2,3,7}-unit with k ≥ 1 non-1 digits has at most
N(k) digits, with N(29) = 3433, N(31) = 3832, N(34) = 4466, N(35) = 4687, N(36) = 4912, N(42) = 6363, N(44) = 6883.

**Corollary C1.** Assume RST[1]. (a) With the enumeration below 10^4000 already in hand, every vertex with k_max ≤ 31
is closed with no modular machinery at all — all of Tiers A and B and the Tier-C vertex 128421199872 — so the
antecedent trees of 2 and 4 are exactly T_2 and T_4 (T_4 = {4, 14, 27, 72, 98, 189, 294, 1161216, 128421199872}), and
every integer with digital root 4 has persistence ≤ 5, with digital root 2 at most 6. (b) With the enumeration
extended to 10^4912 (resp. 10^6883) and no new vertex of k_max > 36 (resp. 44) appearing, the trees of 6 (resp. 8)
are exactly the extended-corpus trees, and persistence is at most 8 (root 6) and 6 (root 8).

**The premise of (b) holds.** Chaffin (OEIS A003001, 2016; benchaffin.com 2019) enumerated every 2^a3^b7^c below
10^{20000} and found that all of them above 10^{140} contain a digit 0; our independent 10-adic sieve of all
448,299,085,464 {2,3,7}-units in [10^{79}, 10^{6885}) — a superset of [10^{80}, 10^{6884}], the range the
corollary needs — (RESULT-SIEVE.md; second certification by Cairn's zfsieve6900, tier0-opt/chk_sieve: per-c counts
equal for all 8147 c, all 5,693,747 stage survivors rechecked; control: exact agreement with the corpus
below 10^{80}; positive control: every corpus element with ≥ 80 digits recovered) finds no zero-free one above 140
digits either. So C′ = C and T′_Δ = T_Δ, and (b) becomes:

**Theorem D (conditional on RST[1]).** Every positive integer with multiplicative digital root 2, 4, 6 or 8 has
persistence at most 6, 5, 8, 6 respectively. Together with Brier–Clavier–Gutsche–Naccache's theorem for the odd
roots (persistence ≤ 2 for roots 1, 3, 7, 9 and ≤ 5 for root 5), every integer whose orbit does not end at 0 has
persistence at most 8.

Calibration: CONDITIONAL(RST[1]) — an ordinary theorem from a named explicit hypothesis plus two finite computations
(the corpus to 10^{4000} and its extension to 10^{6884}, the latter done twice independently); distinct from
everything above, which is unconditional (computation, Lemma C) or conditional only on proved theorems (U″).

**6.6 What remains, exactly (REDUCTION.md, SURVEY-2026-10-21.md).** Sloane's conjecture splits into: odd roots,
including 5 (BCGN); even nonzero roots, i.e. the closure of the 177 vertices (this paper; conditionally complete under
RST[1] once the corpus below 10^{6883} adds no vertex — which Chaffin's exhaustive enumeration of all 2^a3^b7^c below
10^{20000} already asserts and our independent 10-adic sieve certifies); and root 0, equivalent to the absence of
a chain of ten zero-free S-units each the digit product of the previous (Z′), implied by Conjecture Z and of the same
nature — the record 11 is a root-0 chain, and no known method, conditional or not, touches it. For each fixed k the
finiteness of a vertex's children is a theorem (ineffective): 9y = 10^n + Σ9(d_j−1)10^{e_j} − 1 is a (k+3)-term
S-unit equation over ⟨2,3,5,7⟩ with no vanishing subsums, so van der Poorten–Schlickewei/Evertse apply and
Amoroso–Viada bound the number of solutions. Unconditionally, the enumeration of Tier B is blocked by the count of
level-12 parents (≈ 2×10^{15}; §3.11) rather than by any per-child cost, and Tier C by its 10^{22} level-12 patterns;
the final stage, by contrast, is now a certificate for every k ≤ 44 (§3.10).

## 7. What this does not do

Nothing here touches digital root 0 (Conjecture Z, the zero-free S-unit problem) or digital root 5. Nothing bounds
the persistence of an arbitrary integer with even digital root unconditionally: for Δ = 4 the bound rests on the two
unenumerated vertices 1161216 and 128421199872 (Theorem 3), and for Δ ∈ {2, 6, 8} on the 5 closed-below-height and
32 unenumerated vertices of those trees (Table E). Nothing here says anything about persistence 12 beyond the
reduction of §1.1: a number of persistence 12 with even nonzero root would have an iterate that is a child, outside
T_Δ, of one of the vertices not closed here, hence (by the corpus) a number with more than 4000 digits. The sandwich
is a proof conditional on Matveev; the Lean coverage is the two arithmetic cores of §4.7; the sweep files are the
evidence for Theorems 1–2 and are reproducible by `tables.py` from the inputs named at its head. The base-8 and
base-6 theorems are stated only as far as the files support them (§2, Theorems 5–6); the base-8 sandwich on the Mac
is still in progress (22 of 70 vertices processed).

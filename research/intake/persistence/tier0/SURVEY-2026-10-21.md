# Finiteness is proven; effectivity and the lift remain

Every open piece of the project's reduction of Sloane's persistence problem sits in one of three well-mapped regimes, and the literature says something sharp about each. For closing the children of a vertex (zero-free {2,3,7}-units with a fixed multiset of k non-1 digits), **finiteness is already a theorem for every fixed k** via the van der Poorten–Schlickewei/Evertse S-unit theorem, with an Amoroso–Viada count of at most (8(k+2))^{4(k+2)^4(k+7)} solutions ([Levin](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1303.4125); [Amoroso–Viada](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/92f49bf5-bb0c-4d6e-a1b5-1022e0f59195/Duke5.pdf)), and an **unconditional effective bound of shape n ≤ (C·k log k)^k** is within reach by transplanting Bugeaud–Kaneko's iterated-gap Lemma 3.1 ([arXiv:1704.00432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00432)) onto the project's signed representation 9y = 10^n + Σ 9(d_j−1)10^{e_j} − 1; the project's existing Baker/Yu sandwich is exactly the lemma's "one big top gap" case, and the p-adic gap step is the piece it lacks. On conditional bounds, the project's RST[1] choice C₁ = 1 is conservative relative to RST's own heuristic C₁ = β + ε ≈ 0.597 ([RST](https://tenenb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/PPP/abc.pdf)), and **Baker's explicit abc conjecture gives much smaller correction factors whenever ω of the triples is small** (log-factor ≈ 15 versus ≈ 45 at log N = 100, ω = 4) ([Baker 2004](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/972/download/10_5486_PMD_2004_3348.pdf)), so the chain can likely be re-run to shrink N(31) = 3832 and N(44) = 6883. Because both of those are below 20000 digits, the conditional theorem plus Chaffin's exhaustive enumeration of all {2,3,7}- and {3,5,7}-unit digit products below 10^20000 ([Chaffin](https://benchaffin.com/persistence/persistence.html)) already **closes those children conditionally**, provided the enumeration is independently replicated (Chaffin's code is not public). On zero digits, nothing beyond density-one and X^α counting theorems exists, no standard conjecture implies that large 2^a3^b7^c contain a 0, and Erdős's own verdict ("no method at our disposal") still stands ([Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006v4)). On the 12→24 lift, modular subset sum is Õ(m) and conditionally optimal under SETH, Wagner's k-tree does not apply to a multiplicative-coset target, and the practical gains lie in Dimitrov–Howe-style modulus chains, counting DP, stored-survivor sequential filtering and coset-index lookup tables rather than in new asymptotics ([Axiotis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04825); [Dimitrov–Howe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06440)). The ranked agenda at the end lists eight experiments the project has not tried, led by the iterated-gap effective theorem and a certified replication of the 10^20000 corpus.

## Children of a vertex are finite by theorem; the gap is effectivity

The project's children equation, after multiplying by 9, reads 9·2^a3^b7^c = 10^n + Σ_{j=1}^k 9(d_j−1)·10^{e_j} − 1 with d_j ∈ {2,…,9}. This is a (k+3)-term S-unit equation over the rank-4 group ⟨2,3,5,7⟩ with fixed coefficients in {1, 9,…,72, −1}, and since all powers of 10 enter positively, no subsum vanishes for n ≥ 1 (a vanishing subsum containing −9y would force the remaining positive terms to sum to 1). The van der Poorten–Schlickewei and Evertse theorems therefore give **finitely many (n, e_1,…,e_k, a, b, c) for each fixed digit multiset**, ineffectively ([Levin, arXiv:1303.4125](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1303.4125)), and Amoroso–Viada's bound of (8m)^{4m^4(m+r+1)} nondegenerate solutions for an m-term equation in a rank-r group gives, with m = k+2 and r = 4, an explicit but astronomical count ([Amoroso–Viada, Duke](https://iris.unito.it/retrieve/92f49bf5-bb0c-4d6e-a1b5-1022e0f59195/Duke5.pdf); exponent extracted from garbled text and flagged in the notes). A second, independent ineffective route arrived in August 2026: Bugeaud's Theorem 1.2 says that integers whose base-b digit word has linear factor complexity p(ℓ) ≤ Cℓ, and are not too divisible by b, have a prime factor outside any finite S once they are large ([Bugeaud, arXiv:2608.23017](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23017v1)). The digit word 1^n with k substituted letters has complexity at most (k+1)ℓ, so this gives finiteness uniform in both the digit values and their positions, although the hypothesis must be checked against the PDF. The point for the project's write-up is that **the open content of "closing a vertex" is purely the effectivity and size of the bound**, not finiteness.

The effective route runs through linear forms in logarithms, and here the literature contains a tool the project has not used. Bugeaud–Kaneko's Lemma 3.1 handles an S-unit N = d_k b^{n_k} + … + d_1 with k nonzero digits by a two-case pigeonhole over the gap sequence: if n_k ≥ 2n_{k−1}, one archimedean Matveev estimate on |N/(d_k b^{n_k}) − 1| bounds n_k; otherwise one chooses the smallest ℓ with n_{1+ℓ} ≥ n_k^{ℓ/(k−2)}, forms a linear form from the leading and trailing digit blocks, and compares its p-adic valuation (p | b, bounded below by a power of n_k) against Yu's upper bound, yielding **n_k ≤ (c₁ C₁^s k* ∏ log q_i · log(k log q_s))^{k*} with k* = max{k−2, 1}** ([Bugeaud–Kaneko, arXiv:1704.00432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00432)). Only two linear forms are ever used, each in s+1 logarithms; the k-dependence enters through the exponent k−2, not through a growing number of logarithms, which is why the bound is (…)^{k−2} and not a tower. The authors note the argument does not need k fixed and that the p-adic step is the novelty ([arXiv:1704.00432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00432)). Applied to the signed representation above (s = 3 primes, base 10, k+2 "digits" with coefficients bounded by 72, lowest exponent 0 with coefficient −1, so the "not divisible by b" hypothesis holds), the lemma should transfer with 72 replacing b−1 in the height constants, giving n ≤ (c₁ C₁^3 · k · log 2 · log 3 · log 7 · log(k log 7))^k. The Matveev constant 30^{n+3} n^{9/2} and Yu's factor (16e)^{2(n+1)} make the numerical bound far beyond enumeration ([Bugeaud, arXiv:1609.07926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07926)), but it would be a closed, unconditional theorem for every k, with the project's existing CVP sandwich as its top-gap case. Two caveats: the lemma is stated for digits d_i ∈ {1,…,b−1}, so the coefficient generalization needs a line-by-line check, and since 2 ∈ S the p-adic form should be taken 5-adically (or the 2-power handled separately, as the project's 2-adic descent already does). For k = 1 the sharper two-case argument in Bugeaud's Osaka paper (Theorem 4.3 on a^m + b^n + 1) applies directly and gives n ≤ c·C^s·Q·log Q·log A ([arXiv:1609.07926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07926)).

Several other advertised techniques do not fit, and the reasons are instructive. Effective S-unit equation theorems with four or more terms exist only for |S| ≤ 3 (Vojta's three-unknown theorem; Bajpai–Bennett's five-term theorem), whereas the target needs the five places {∞, 2, 3, 5, 7}; Bajpai–Bennett themselves say that for n ≥ 4 terms, effective results "remain, in almost every situation, beyond current technology" ([arXiv:2308.05162](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.05162)). Corvaja–Zannier's Subspace-plus-Padé method for odd perfect powers with four binary digits is a perfect-power technique whose authors explicitly cannot handle five digits or base 3 ([AIF 2013](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.2774.pdf)). Cobham-type theorems (Albayrak–Bell's quantitative version bounds intersections of sparse automatic sets) apply only when both sets are automatic, and {2^a3^b7^c} is automatic in no base; they cover only the single-prime subcases S = {2}, {3}, {7} ([arXiv:2304.09223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09223)). Lattice methods are more subtle. De Weger's real approximation lattice reduces the Matveev bound on the top archimedean form |9·2^a3^b7^c·10^{−n} − 1| to enumeration scale, which is what the project's CVP step does ([de Weger thesis](https://www.win.tue.nl/~bdeweger/proefschrift.html)); but **p-adic LLL from the bottom of the expansion is vacuous**, because the 5-adic form v_5(2^a3^b7^c·L^{−1} − 1) ≥ e_k has a "constant" L of height ≈ e_k log 10, so Yu's bound v_5 ≤ C·h(L)·log B says nothing unless L is enumerated, which is the project's 10-adic pinning. The usable p-adic forms are the Bugeaud–Kaneko gap forms, and those become fixed-α forms only after the low block is pinned; at that point Pethő–de Weger's p-adic "Algorithm A", as used in Pink–Ziegler's pipeline (Matveev → p-adic → real LLL → p-adic reduction → brute force, taking a Fibonacci instance from 2.6·10^117 to n ≤ 2300 in 4.5 hours), can shrink the enumeration of the next gap ([Pink–Ziegler, arXiv:1604.04720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04720)). Note that R_n = (10^n−1)/9 is the recurrence u_n = 11u_{n−1} − 10u_{n−2} with roots 10 and 1, which is exactly Pink–Ziegler's exceptional case β = ±1, so their theorem does not literally cover the equation even for k = 1; only their algorithmic template transfers.

Dimitrov–Howe's computation is the closest published analogue of the project's residue sweeps and contains two importable ideas. They found all powers of 3 with at most 22 nonzero bits by solving 3^x ≡ 2^{a_1} + … + 2^{a_n} along a chain of moduli M_1 | M_2 | … with 64 factors, choosing primes p ≡ 1 mod 2^a3^b so that the order ratios O_2(M_i)/O_2(M_{i−1}) stay in {1, 2}, stopping when every 2^{a_j} is "determinate" (2^{a_j+1} | M), after which modular solutions lift uniquely to ℤ; each lift step costs ∏ #A_j (discrete-log test, unbalanced case) or roughly √(#X·∏#A_j) (sorted-list meet-in-the-middle, balanced case), and the n = 22 run took 743,737 process-seconds in Magma ([Dimitrov–Howe, arXiv:2105.06440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06440)). They contrast this with the Stewart-type effective bound B(22) > 4.9·10^46, which illustrates the gap between Baker-scale and enumeration-scale bounds ([arXiv:2105.06440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06440)). The determinate-power criterion is the project's 10-adic pinning in another guise; the chain-ordering heuristic and the balanced/unbalanced switch are not yet in the project's toolkit.

## Explicit abc variants: Baker's 6/5 beats RST[1] when ω is small

The project's conditional theorem rests on RST's Conjecture A, c < k·exp(4√(3 log k/log₂k)·(1 + log₃k/(2 log₂k) + C₁/log₂k)) with k = rad(abc) and an unspecified real C₁ ([RST, Bull. LMS 2014](https://tenenb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/PPP/abc.pdf)). RST's heuristic Conjecture C (assuming only that k(a+b) is statistically independent of k(a), k(b)) implies Conjecture A with **C₁ = β + ε, where β = 1 + log 3 − (13/6) log 2 ≈ 0.597**, so the project's C₁ = 1 is the safe side of RST's own prediction, and nothing in the literature licenses an "official" smaller value ([RST](https://tenenb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/PPP/abc.pdf)). The exponent floor is a theorem: Stewart–Tijdeman and van Frankenhuijsen prove infinitely many coprime triples with c > k·exp(6.068·√(log k)/log log k), so no bound c < K·rad(abc) with a constant K can hold and the per-triple exponent must be 1 + o(1) with o(1) ≳ const/√(log R log log R) ([RST](https://tenenb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/PPP/abc.pdf); [Martin–Miao](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.2974)). The best unconditional inequality, Stewart–Yu's c < exp(K₇·R^{1/3}(log R)³), is useless for digit counts because R grows with 10^{e_j} ([Martin–Miao](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.2974)).

The alternative the project has not exploited is Baker's explicit conjecture, the only abc variant with fully numerical constants: **max(|a|,|b|,|c|) < (6/5)·N·(log N)^ω/ω!** with N = rad(abc) and ω = ω(N), calibrated against 196 extremal triples lying along c ≈ 1.1998·N(log N)^ω/ω!, with Baker judging "the constant 6/5 … relatively secure" ([Baker 2004, Publ. Math. Debrecen](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/972/download/10_5486_PMD_2004_3348.pdf)). Laishram–Shorey derive from it the uniform c < N^{7/4} and a table of (ε, ω_ε, N_ε) thresholds for c < κ_ε·N^{1+ε}, e.g. (1/2, 127, e^{679.6}) and (1/3, 6460, e^{63727}) ([arXiv:1112.2461](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.2461)). The comparison matters: at log N = 100 the RST[1] correction is exp(≈ 44.7) while Baker's is exp(≈ 15.2) for ω = 4 and exp(≈ 31) for ω = 10, so **for triples whose radical is built from {2,3,5,7} plus a few gap primes, Baker's conjecture should give noticeably smaller N(k)**. The cost is that Baker's bound needs ω of each triple, which depends on the unknown gap factors; the uniform N^{7/4} fallback is far weaker than RST for large N. A chain that uses Baker's bound where ω is controlled and RST[1] elsewhere is the natural experiment. Whether 6/5 survives the 9.3 million ABC@home triples with 10^18 ≤ c < 2^63 found after 2004 is unverified here; a 2019 Debrecen paper reportedly re-tests it, and the Leiden tables are the data source ([Leiden abc list](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~smitbde/abc/?set=1)).

Structurally the cleanest conditional statement would treat the whole (k+3)-term equation at once. Its terms are all {2,3,5,7}-units, so the radical of their product is at most 210 and the gcd is 1 because of the term −1; the Browkin–Brzeziński n-conjecture, max|a_i| ≤ B(n,ε)·rad(∏a_i)^{2n−5+ε} under the hypotheses a_1+…+a_n = 0, gcd = 1 and no vanishing {0,1}-subsum, would then give **10^n ≤ B(k+3,ε)·210^{2k+1+ε}, i.e. n ≲ 2.32·(2k+1) + log₁₀B — about 146 + log₁₀B digits for k = 31 versus the project's 3832** ([Martin–Miao](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.2974); [arXiv:2409.13439v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13439v2)). This is not usable: no version of the n-conjecture has an explicit constant, and the exponent 2n−5 is proven sharp (Browkin–Brzeziński showed the limsup quality is at least 2n−5 for every n ≥ 3), so the per-gap chain is the only explicit conditional framework available ([arXiv:2409.13439v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13439v2)). The subsum-nondegeneracy argument also deserves a careful check in the project's own write-up, since no source performs it for this equation. Finally, Lagarias's work belongs under counting, not conditional bounds: his theorems are upper bounds on exceptional exponents (X^{0.9725} for the real system, 2X^{log₃2} for the 3-adic one) with no conjectural input and no effective method ([Lagarias, arXiv:math/0512006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006v4)).

Unconditionally, Bugeaud–Kaneko's Corollary 1.5 is the strongest effective digit statement: an S-unit n ≥ n₀(b,S,ε) not divisible by b has more than (1−ε)·log log n/log log log n nonzero base-b digits, with n₀ effectively computable but never written down numerically ([arXiv:1704.00432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00432)). These theorems bound nonzero digits from below; a zero-free number has the maximum number of nonzero digits, so they are vacuous for the digit-0 question and bite only through the (k+3)-term rewriting, where the expected shape is log log(10^n) ≲ (k+1)(c + log k + …), i.e. n ≲ exp(C·k log k), exponentially weaker than any abc-conditional bound at k ≈ 31–44.

## Zero digits: density-one theorems, X^0.63 counting, and no method

The strongest proven statement about zero digits in the project's family is a density statement. De Faria–Tresser prove that the circle map T_{p,q} is conjugate to rotation by log_q p, so that for log_q p irrational the exponents n with a zero digit in p^n have asymptotic density 1 (Corollary 1), and their Theorem 2 extends this to products of multiplicatively independent primes times any divisor of the base ([de Faria–Tresser, arXiv:1307.1188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.1188)). Since 1, log₁₀2, log₁₀3, log₁₀7 are ℚ-linearly independent, zero-free {2,3,7}-units have density zero among all such units, and that is the end of what Weyl equidistribution delivers. Dupuy–Weirich's result that the low-order base-q digits of p^n are equidistributed on Cesàro average over n (now generalized to number fields by Li–Zhao) is the trailing-digit analogue and likewise says nothing about any individual n; their unproved non-averaged Conjecture 1.2 would imply Erdős's ternary conjecture ([Li–Zhao, arXiv:2601.12753](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12753v1); [Erdős Problem #406 thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/406)). De Faria–Tresser say explicitly that digit-block equidistribution (their Conjecture 3, which would settle Sloane's conjecture in every base) has a step "beyond our reach at this writing" ([arXiv:1307.1188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.1188)).

Counting results are the second family. Narkiewicz bounded the number of n ≤ X with 2^n omitting ternary digit 2 by 1.62·X^{log₃2}, and Lagarias extended this to N_λ(X) ≤ 25·X^{0.9725} for ⌊λ2^n⌋ and 2·X^{α₀}, α₀ = log₃2 ≈ 0.63092, for 3-adic multipliers, with Hausdorff dimension results for the exceptional sets and the remark that Erdős's conjecture "cannot be resolved with a finite computation" ([Lagarias](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512006v4); [erdosproblems.com/406](https://www.erdosproblems.com/406)). The exponent is the dimension of the digit-omitting Cantor set, so these methods save at most a power of the exponent and can never give finiteness; in base 10 the zero-omitting set has dimension log₁₀9 ≈ 0.954 and 2 is not a unit mod 10, and for a three-parameter family there is no single orbit to count along. Maynard's circle-method proof that infinitely many primes omit any given decimal digit runs in the opposite direction, locating a dense sequence inside a set of size X^{0.954}, and has no purchase on a sequence with (log X)³ elements ([Maynard, arXiv:1604.01041](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01041)). Bugeaud 2018 and Bugeaud–Kaneko confirm in their own scope statements that they count nonzero digits only ([arXiv:1609.07926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07926v1); [arXiv:1704.00432](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.00432v1)). No source connects abc, Lang–Waldschmidt, Schanuel or GRH to the zero-digit statement; the absence is inferred from the silence of Lagarias, BBM and the Erdős Problems page, and the structural reason is that "y has a 0 somewhere" has no few-term S-unit identity, so abc-type conjectures bite only in the fixed-k setting the project already uses.

Computational records are extensive and should be cited precisely. For powers of 2 in base 10, OEIS A007377 records that 86 is conjecturally the last zero-free exponent, checked to k = 10^10 by Radcliffe in August 2022, with A020665 giving conjectured last zero-free exponents 3^68, 7^35 and others ([OEIS A007377](https://oeis.org/A007377); [OEIS A020665](https://oeis.org/A020665)). In base 3, Saye verified both Erdős's and Sloane's ternary conjectures for **n ≤ 2·3^45 ≈ 5.9·10^21** by a depth-first construction of powers of 2 with prescribed trailing ternary digits (depth 46, arithmetic mod 3^54, Θ(2^K) candidates instead of Θ(3^K)), and reports an outlier n = 201015414581294 with 98 trailing non-2 digits ([Saye, JIS 2022](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL25/Saye/saye3.pdf)). Dimitrov–Howe showed the only powers of 2 that are sums of at most 25 distinct powers of 3 are 2^0, 2^2, 2^8 ([arXiv:2105.06440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06440)). For the project's own family the relevant corpus is Chaffin's: enumerating all 7^i3^j2^k and 7^i3^j5^k below 10^20000 with staged zero checks (mod 10^9, 10^19, 10^76, then full precision, catching a zero 99.96% of the time), he found that **the largest zero-free digit product with persistence ≥ 2 is the 140-digit 2^25·3^227·7^28 and every product between 10^140 and 10^20000 contains a zero**, with 11994 persistence-2 products in total ([Chaffin](https://benchaffin.com/persistence/persistence.html); [OEIS A003001](https://oeis.org/A003001)). McElwee's independent search to 10^525 found no new tree members after 10^140 ([McElwee](https://kmcelwee.medium.com/multiplicative-persistence-is-solved-1937692b26cc)). Our own enumeration in this research pass of all 11,119,028 {2,3,7}-units below 10^200 (unpublished, scratch script) found 8807 zero-free ones with the same 140-digit maximum, and a digit-independence model N(d)·0.9^{d−2} predicts 8985, matching length by length (d = 20: 255 predicted vs 256 observed; d = 80: 7.2 vs 7) and giving an expected 0.41 zero-free units with ≥ 141 digits and about 8·10^{−906} with ≥ 20001 digits. Chaffin's own version of this heuristic puts the no-zero probability per 20000-digit candidate below 10^{−915} while stressing that the search "does not prove that 11 is the maximum" ([Chaffin](https://benchaffin.com/persistence/persistence.html)). No OEIS sequence or paper lists zero-free 2^a3^b7^c as such, and the name "Conjecture Z" appears nowhere in the sources; it is the project's own terminology.

Two points matter for the project. First, Chaffin's and Peters' corpora are different objects: Chaffin's result is a statement about **all** {2,3,7}- and {3,5,7}-units below 10^20000, whereas Peters enumerated the (8N²+13N+6)(N+1)/6 canonical digit strings up to 30000 digits and observed that every digit product with at least 20000 digits has a zero within its last 306 digits (his example 2^13802·3^16807·7^1757 has 13659 digits, 1335 zeros, and ends in a zero followed by 305 nonzero digits) ([OEIS A003001](https://oeis.org/A003001)). Second, the Kurz 2002 record on A014553 exceeded Carmody, Diamond and De Comité by orders of magnitude yet is uncited by Perez–Styer and Delahaye, and Radcliffe's 20585 cannot be reconstructed from the naive argument (which gives 20959), so both should be checked against the comment text before citing.

The theoretical side of persistence is thinner. Brier–Clavier–Gutsche–Naccache proved Ξ(n) ≤ 1 for terminal digits in {1,3,7,9} and Ξ(n) ≤ 5 for terminal digit 5, by reducing each antecedent-tree vertex to equations 2^h(10^{a_0} + 18Σc_i10^{a_i}) + τ_h = 3^u7^w solved modulo 2^9·5^6, m₁₂ = (10^12−1)/(3^3·7), m₂₄, m₄₈ and primes 73, 137, 94031, 17, 98071, which is the ancestry of the project's level-12/24/48/96 residue patterns ([BCGN, arXiv:2110.04263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04263v1)). For even targets they tabulate 33, 9, 84 and 51 vertices with 1117, 1062, 6377 and 4774 equation families and maximal term counts 30, 32, 37 and 45, call exhausting 12^30 ≈ 2^107 tuples "totally out of reach", and state that target 0 "can not be treated by our method" ([arXiv:2110.04263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.04263v1)). Fonga (August 2026) proved their Conjecture 2: for fixed multiplicities ν₂,…,ν₉ and arbitrarily many 1s, **v₂(x) ≤ ⌊ρ·c^q + ((c^q−1)/(c−1))·log₂B⌋** with c = log₂10, B = 1 + 9Σ(d−1)ν_d and ρ ∈ {0,1,2,3} by which even digits occur, with an exact recursive maximum (Theorem 4.1) and a general version for any base b ≥ 3 and prime p | b; the resulting finite algorithm for nonzero even targets is described but not executed ([Fonga, arXiv:2608.27802](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27802v1)). The structural picture is therefore: odd targets are a theorem, nonzero even targets are reduced to an unexecuted finite computation, and target 0, the overwhelmingly common endpoint, has only density theorems and computations. Lamont-Smith's cross-base tables and Perez–Styer's per-base theorems supply data (base-10 maximum 11; prime bases trend higher; factorial base unbounded by Diamond–Reidpath) but no closure technique ([Lamont-Smith, JIS 2021](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL24/Lamont/lamont5.pdf); [OEIS A208277](https://oeis.org/A208277)).

## The lift is memory-bound: Õ(m) is optimal and k-tree does not apply

The project's additive reformulation of the lift kills asks for all subsets S of q ≤ 45 terms whose sum Δ_S lands in an affine image of a multiplicative coset modulo m. The reachability half of this problem is solved by the modular subset-sum literature: Koiliaris–Xu compute all realizable sums in Õ(min{√n·m, m^{5/4}}), Axiotis et al. reach Õ(m) (O(m log^7 m) w.h.p.) by linear sketching, Cardinal–Iacono get O(m log m) w.h.p. by running Bellman's recurrence σ_i = σ_{i−1} OR rotate(σ_{i−1}, s_i) on a dynamic-string structure, and Potępa gives deterministic O(m log m·α(m)); every one of these computes **the full set of attainable residues at once, so a target set A costs nothing extra** ([Koiliaris–Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02318); [Axiotis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.04825); [Cardinal–Iacono](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.08417); [Potępa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06062)). Under SETH, Bellman's O*(T) cannot be improved to T^{1−ε}·2^{o(n)} ([Abboud et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04546)), which Axiotis et al. cite as the matching lower bound for the modular case. With q ≤ 45 and m ≤ 10^5 in the dense stage, the bitset DP's q·m/64 ≈ 7·10^4 word-operations is already below the log factors of the Õ(m) algorithms, so **the project's DP is the practical optimum for the small-modulus stage**; a counting DP (integers per residue rather than bits, still O(q·m)) additionally yields the exact output size per residue before enumeration, allowing a per-instance choice between backtracking at O(N_out·q) and meet-in-the-middle at O(2^{q/2} + N_out). No source gives an output-sensitive enumeration bound tighter than O(q·m + N_out·q) for a target set, and the SETH bound rules out anything sublinear in m with subexponential dependence on q.

Generalized-birthday methods do not transfer. Wagner's 4-list algorithm runs in O(2^{n/3}) only when the lists can be grown freely and the join condition is prefix-linear (low bits of L₁+L₂ equal a constant); he states that the algorithm "works only when one can extend the size of the lists freely" and treats a single constant target, not a target set ([Wagner, CRYPTO 2002](https://www.iacr.org/archive/crypto2002/24420288/24420288.pdf)). A coset condition V + wΔ ∈ x·H is decided by a character χ(V + wΔ) = 1, which is nonlinear in Δ, so the first-level join cannot discard pairs and the method degrades to plain meet-in-the-middle at 2^{q/2} per target residue, worse than the DP for all practical (q, m) in the project. Dinur's time–memory tradeoffs for K ≥ 8 lists (T³M = N for K = 8) and the Howgrave-Graham–Joux/Becker–Coron–Joux representation technique (O(2^{0.291n}) for random density-1 knapsacks with a single target) share the same obstruction ([Dinur, eprint 2018/575](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/575); [BCJ, eprint 2011/474](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/474)). The Product Subset Problem literature, which is linear in the log domain, confirms that purely multiplicative constraints are handled by birthday attacks and offers nothing for the mixed additive/multiplicative case ([Draziotis et al., arXiv:2002.07095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.07095)).

What the literature does supply is a cost model for combining primes and a Fourier description of survival rates. Dimitrov–Howe's sequential lifting stores survivors modulo M_{i−1} and re-filters them modulo M_i, avoiding CRT blow-up automatically; in the project's setting the terms are fixed residues (lift multiplicity 1), so each step is one coset-membership test per survivor per prime, and the total cost is Σ_i N_i over the survivor counts ([Dimitrov–Howe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06440)). The survivor count at a single prime has the exact Fourier expression 2^q·|A_p|/p + (1/p)Σ_{a≠0} \hat{1}_A(a)·e(aV/p)·∏_j(1 + e(a w t_j/p)), whose error term is governed by |∏_j 2cos(π a w t_j/p)| and is exponentially small in q unless a·w·t_j/p clusters near 0 or 1/2; the heuristic N_i ≈ 2^q·∏_{j≤i}(|A_{p_j}|/p_j) is folklore equidistribution and **no theorem proves it for arbitrary term sets filtered by multiplicative cosets** (the only equidistribution results found concern t-subsets of {1,…,n} or h-subsets of a whole group) ([Wilf–Wagon](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v1i1r3); [Wang, arXiv:2505.12319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12319)). Olson-type bounds, |Σ(S)| ≥ |S|(|S|+2)/4 − 1 for symmetric S with aperiodic sumset, only constrain the regime q ≳ 2√p, far above the project's q ≤ 45 at p ≈ 10^8 ([arXiv:1112.1929](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1929)).

Hardware figures set the practical limits. No published GPU implementation of the modular bitset shift-OR DP with per-item throughput exists in the sources; the GPU subset-sum papers implement two-list meet-in-the-middle at 4–9× over sequential for n = 42–54 ([Wan et al.](https://www.cs.otago.ac.nz/pmam2014/slides/LanjunWan.pdf)). At p ≈ 10^8 a q×p bit table is about 560 MB and each item is a bandwidth-bound streaming pass of p/8 bytes, so a per-instance cost near 1 ms makes ~10^14 instances infeasible at that modulus; the small-m dense DP must do nearly all the filtering first. For the membership tests themselves, a complete coset-index table at p ≈ 10^8 (one or two bytes per entry) makes each test a single memory access, far cheaper than Bernstein–Lange's ≈ 1.77·ℓ^{1/3} ≈ 820 multiplications per log, which becomes relevant only for primes ≳ 10^11 where a table no longer fits ([Bernstein–Lange, eprint 2012/458](https://eprint.iacr.org/2012/458)); Henry–Goldberg's CUDA Pohlig–Hellman solver (840 M 192-bit modular multiplications per second per 2011 Tesla M2050) is the only measured GPU discrete-log work and targets 1536-bit moduli ([cudadl](https://cypherpunks.ca/~iang/pubs/cudadl.pdf)).

Several tempting directions should be dropped with a sentence of justification each. Wagner k-tree and representation-technique knapsack solvers need prefix-linear conditions and freely growable lists, neither of which the coset target provides; p-adic LLL from the bottom of the expansion is vacuous because the low block's height matches the valuation being bounded; effective S-unit equation theorems with ≥ 4 terms require |S| ≤ 3 and the target needs five places; Cobham-type theorems need automatic sets and {2^a3^b7^c} is automatic in no base; Maynard's sieve locates dense sequences in thin digit sets, the reverse of what is needed; and no chain of Stewart–Yu bounds gives digit information because the radical grows with the gap exponents. The n-conjecture framing (n ≲ 2.32(2k+1) + log₁₀B) is worth one paragraph in the write-up as the "ideal conditional bound" that no explicit-constant conjecture currently supports.

## Conclusion

The survey shifts the project's self-description. The children of every vertex are finite by theorem, and an explicit unconditional bound is one lemma-transplant away; what remains genuinely open is the distance between Baker-scale bounds (astronomical) and enumeration-scale bounds (thousands of digits), and that distance is bridged only by abc-type hypotheses or by computation. On the conditional side, the project has been using a defensible but conservative hypothesis: RST's own heuristic argues for C₁ ≈ 0.6, and Baker's numerically calibrated conjecture is stronger still for low-ω triples, so N(31) and N(44) are likely overestimates by a meaningful margin. The most consequential realization is that those bounds already lie below the 10^20000 reach of Chaffin's exhaustive enumeration, so a certified replication of that corpus turns the conditional theorem into a conditional closure with no further search.

On the two fronts the literature cannot help, the verdict is equally clear. Target 0 is where the conjecture lives, every expert source from Erdős to de Faria–Tresser to Fonga calls it out of reach, and the heuristic model fits the data so well (predicted 8985 versus 8807 zero-free units below 10^200) that computation can only add confidence, never proof. For the lift, the honest conclusion is that the project is already at the conditional algorithmic optimum for the dense stage; the remaining wins are engineering (modulus ordering, survivor storage, lookup tables, GPU streaming) and a rigorous survival-rate theorem for structured term sets, which would be a small but genuinely new contribution to additive combinatorics.

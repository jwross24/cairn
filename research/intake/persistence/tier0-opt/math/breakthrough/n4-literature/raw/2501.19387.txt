                                                                                 Abstract
                                                      The exponential local-global principle, or Skolem conjecture, says:
                                                Suppose that b is a positive integer, and that the sequence (un )∞    n=−∞ is
                                                such that every term is in Z[1/b], the linear recurrence un+d = a1 un+d−1 +
                                                · · ·+ad un holds for all integers n, and every root of xd −a1 xd−1 −a2 xd−2 −
                                                · · · − ad is nonzero and simple; then there is no zero term un if and only
                                                if, for some integer m that is larger than 1 and relatively prime to b, every
                                                term un is not in mZ[1/b].
                                                      Particular cases of the conjecture are known, but the general con-
                                                jecture is open. This paper proves some apparently new quadratic and
                                                degenerate cubic cases of the exponential local-global principle via power
                                                residue symbols.
                                                      This work was presented at the Stellenbosch Number Theory Confer-
                                                ence 2025 in January 2025 at Stellenbosch University; much of the work
                                                was also presented at the 67th Annual Congress of the South African
                                                Mathematical Society in December 2024 at the University of Pretoria.
                                              Keywords: Recurrence; local-global principle; power residue symbol; number

1    Introduction
The exponential local-global principle, also called the Skolem conjecture, says
(see [2]):
Conjecture 1.1 (Exponential local-global principle). Let a1 , . . . , ad ∈ Q be
such that ad 6= 0 and all roots of the polynomial f (x) = xd − a1 xd−1 − a2 xd−2 −
· · · − ad are simple. Suppose that b is a positive integer, and that the sequence
u = (un )∞ n=−∞ is such that every term is in Z[1/b] and, for all n ∈ Z,

                         un+d = a1 un+d−1 + · · · + ad un .

There is no zero term un if and only if, for some integer m ⩾ 2, we have
gcd(b, m) = 1 and every term un is not in mZ[1/b].                    
    If there is a zero term in u, then the condition about m must clearly fail.
The interesting part of the conjecture is the inverse – the statement that if there
is no zero term in u, then the condition about m holds. The conjecture is open
in general.
    The assumption that the roots of f (x) are simple is needed, as the following
example illustrates.
Example 1.2. (See [2].) Consider the sequence u in Z[1/2] given by

                           un = (2n + 1)2n for n ∈ Z.

We have un+2 = 4un+1 − 4un for n ∈ Z, and

                         f (x) = x2 − 4x + 4 = (x − 2)2 .

No term of u is zero. However, for each odd positive integer m, we have m | un
for every integer n of the form n = km + (m − 1)/2 with k ∈ Z.               
    For each prime p, write vp (x) for the p-adic valuation of a nonzero rational
number x (for example, vp (pn ) = n for n ∈ Z). We say that a prime p is coprime
to a nonzero rational number x if and only if vp (x) = 0.
    It is straightforward to prove the exponential local-global principle in the
linear case, that is, in the case where d = 1.
Proposition 1.3. The exponential local-global principle holds in the case where
d = 1.
Proof. Consider a sequence u of numbers in Z[1/b] where, for some a1 ∈ Q−{0},
we have un+1 = a1 un for n ∈ Z. Clearly, un = u0 an1 for all n ∈ Z. If u0 = 0,
then all terms of u are zero and the conjecture holds. If u0 6= 0, then no term
of u is zero and each prime p that is coprime to both a1 and u0 satisfies un 6≡ 0
(mod p) for all n ∈ Z, so the conjecture holds (and all but finitely many primes
work as choices for m).

    The major goal of this paper is to prove a strengthening of the exponential
local-global principle in many cases where d = 2 and f (x) has two distinct
rational roots. From this, some degenerate cases where d = 3 will quickly
follow.
    This work was presented at the Stellenbosch Number Theory Conference
2025 in January 2025 at Stellenbosch University; much of the work was also
presented at the 67th Annual Congress of the South African Mathematical So-
ciety in December 2024 at the University of Pretoria. This paper covers the first
year of a planned three-year research project associated with the University of
Pretoria’s Research Development Programme; for the second and third years of
the project, the aim is to prove the exponential local-global principle in other
cases (including families of cases where f (x) is cubic or quartic), and to examine
whether and when the principle holds if algebraic number fields other than Q
are considered.
    In this article, from now on until the section on degenerate cubic cases, we
assume that d = 2, we assume that the numbers a1 , a2 in Q are such that
a2 6= 0 and a21 + 4a2 6= 0, we let b be a positive integer, we consider a sequence
u = (un )∞
         n=−∞ of numbers in Z[1/b] such that

                               un+2 = a1 un+1 + a2 un

for all n ∈ Z, and we assume that the roots c1 and c2 of f (x) = x2 − a1 x − a2
are rational. By Viete’s formulas, c1 c2 = −a2 6= 0. The condition a21 + 4a2 6= 0
yields c1 6= c2 , so by a well known result, there are b1 , b2 ∈ Q such that, for
n ∈ Z, we have un = b1 cn1 + b2 cn2 . If 0 ∈ {b1 , b2 } then u ≡ 0 or we are in a d = 1
case; from now on, let us assume b1 6= 0 and b2 6= 0. We write B = −b2 /b1 and
C = c1 /c2 .
    Under those assumptions, we shall prove the following main theorem.
Theorem 1.4. Suppose that
 (1) We have C m = B for some m ∈ Z, or
 (2) We have (C, B) = (1, −1), or
 (3) We have B k C ℓ ∈
                     / {±1} for all k ∈ Z − {0} and all ℓ ∈ Z.
There is no zero term in u if and only if, for infinitely many primes p, we have
gcd(b, p) = 1 and every term un is not divisible by p.                        
   To prove Theorem 1.4, we shall use a Chebotarev-density-like result of
Schinzel [4] on power residue symbols. Unlike some articles of Schinzel [4, 5]
that proved some quadratic cases of the exponential local-global principle, the
number a2 is not necessarily ±1 in our work; Theorem 1.4 is apparently a new
result.

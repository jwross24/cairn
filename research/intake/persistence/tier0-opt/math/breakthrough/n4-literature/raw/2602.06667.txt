                                             We recall that a linear recurrence sequence (un )n≥0 of order k over a number
                                          field K is a sequence which satisfies a relation of the form
                                                              un+k = Ak−1 un+k−1 + · · · + A0 un ,        n ≥ 0,
                                          with some constants A0 , . . . , Ak−1 , u0 , . . . , uk−1 ∈ K. Let α1 , . . . , αq be the
                                          distinct roots of the corresponding characteristic polynomial
                                          (1)                    F (X) := X k − Ak−1 X k−1 − · · · − A0 .
                                          Then for n ≥ 0, we have
                                          (2)                         un = f1 (n)α1n + · · · + fq (n)αqn ,
                                          where fi (n) (i = 1, . . . , q) are non-zero polynomials with degree less than the
                                          multiplicity of αi ; the coefficients of fi (n) are elements of the number field
                                          Q(α1 , . . . , αq ) (see [19, Theorem C.1]).
                                            The sequence (un )n≥0 is called degenerate if there are integers i, j with 1 ≤
                                          i < j ≤ q such that αi /αj is a root of unity; otherwise it is called non-
                                          degenerate. The sequence (un )n≥0 is called simple if q = k. In this case, (2)
                                          becomes
                                          (3)                            un = f1 α1n + · · · + fk αkn ,
                                          where fi (i = 1, . . . , k) are constants.

   For any integer m, let P (m) denote the greatest prime factor of m and
Q(m) denote the greatest square-free factor of m with the convention that
P (0) = P (±1) = 1 and Q(0) = Q(±1) = 1. That is, if m = ph1 1 . . . phr r
with p1 < · · · < pr primes and h1 , . . . , hr positive integers, then P (m) =
pr and Q(m) = p1 . . . pr . In 1935, Mahler [10] showed that for any non-
degenerate linear recurrence sequence (un )n≥0 , the absolute value of its terms
grows without bound; more precisely,
(4)                          |un | −→ ∞ as n −→ ∞.
Later, van der Poorten and Schlickewei [22], as well as Evertse [7], using a
p-adic version of Schmidt’s subspace theorem due to Schlickewei [17], proved
that if (un )n≥0 is a non-degenerate linear recurrence sequence, then the greatest
prime factor of un tends to infinity, namely
(5)                         P (un ) −→ ∞ as n −→ ∞.
Both results are ineffective in the sense that they do not yield explicit bounds.
In contrast, if the characteristic polynomial of (un )n≥0 admits a dominant root,
that is,
                          |α1 | > |αj |   (j = 2, . . . , q),
then one obtains an effective lower bound of the form
                                  |un | > c1 nℓ1 |α1 |n
for all n > c2 , where c1 is equal to one half of the absolute value of the
coefficient of xℓ1 in the polynomial f1 , and c2 is a positive constant that can
be computed effectively in terms of α1 , . . . , αq and f1 , . . . , fq .
   In 1982, Stewart [21] derived effective lower bounds for both the greatest
prime factor and the greatest square-free factor of un in the case where the
characteristic polynomial admits a dominant root. More precisely, assuming
that un ̸= f1 (n)α1n , for any ε > 0 one has
(6)                             P (un ) > (1 − ε) log n
and
(7)                                 Q(un ) > n1−ε ,
for all n > c3 , where c3 is a positive constant that can be computed effectively
in terms of ε and the parameters α1 , . . . , αq and f1 , . . . , fq . These bounds were
obtained by applying a version of Baker’s theory on linear forms in logarithms
of algebraic numbers due to Waldschmidt [23]. Independently, Shparlinski [18]
established the estimate for P (un ) in the special case where f1 (n) is a non-zero
constant, with the exponent 1−ε replaced by a suitably small positive constant.
Later, both inequalities were further refined using a result of Matveev [12],
                                                                                    3

which gives explicit lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms of algebraic
numbers.
  Let S = {p1 , . . . , ps } be a finite, non-empty set of distinct prime numbers.
For an integer m, its S-part, denoted by [m]S , is defined as
                                 [m]S = pr11 · · · prss ,
where m = pr11 · · · prss M with M coprime to each pi for i = 1, . . . , s, and
r1 , . . . , rs are non-negative integers. Using a p-adic extension of Roth’s theorem
due to Ridout [16], Mahler [11] showed that for certain classes of binary linear
recurrence sequences (un )n≥0 , we have the following
                                    [un ]S < |un |ε
for all sufficiently large n. He also noted that this estimate implies that the
greatest prime divisor P (un ) tends to infinity as n → ∞. Subsequently, under
different sets of hypotheses on the sequence (un )n≥0 , Bugeaud and Evertse [2]
obtained effective, though weaker, bounds for the S-part of un of the shape
|un |1−c . For other related work, one can refer to (for example, [3, 4, 9]).
   It is a classical consequence of the Skolem-Mahler-Lech theorem [6] that the
zero set of a linear recurrence sequence (un )n≥0 , namely the set n ∈ N for
which un = 0, consists of the union of a finite set and finitely many arithmetic
progressions. In particular, although strong bounds-uniform in all parameters
and depending only on the order k are known for the total number of zeros
(see [1]), no effective bound is available for the size of an index n satisfying
un = 0. A parametric variant of this problem, concerning families of linear
recurrence sequences, was investigated by Ostafe and Shparlinski [14]. For all
but finitely many values of the parameter for which the specialized sequence
is non-degenerate, they established an explicit upper bound for the largest
possible zero. As a consequence, they showed that the Skolem problem is
effectively decidable outside a set of parameter values α ∈ Q of bounded
height. More precisely, they considered linear recurrence sequences of the
form
                                 k

(8)                   Un (X) =          fi (X)αi (X)n ,     n ≥ 0,
                                  i=1

where fi (X), αi (X) ∈ Q(X). Furthermore, they analyzed other properties of
the specialized values Un (ζ) when the parameter is restricted to the set U of
all the roots of unity.
   In this paper, we study the arithmetic properties of parametric linear re-
currence sequences obtained by specializing the parameter at roots of unity.
Our first main result establishes strong lower bound for the largest prime ideal
divisor and for the radical of the principal ideal generated by the values Un (ζ).

More precisely, under suitable non-degeneracy and non-exceptionality assump-
tions on the characteristic roots, we prove that for all but finitely many roots
of unity ζ, both the greatest prime ideal divisor P (Un (ζ)) grow at least loga-
rithmically with n and the norm of the radical Q(Un (ζ)) grow at least power
of n, with effectively computable constants. Our second result complements
this result by providing an effective upper bound for the norm of the S-part
of Un (ζ), showing that for sufficiently large n it is strictly smaller than a fixed
power of the absolute norm of Un (ζ).

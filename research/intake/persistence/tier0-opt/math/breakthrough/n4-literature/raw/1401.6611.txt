   We also give an application of Theorem 4 to primitive roots modulo

                          2. Preparations
2.1. Notation and general conventions. Throughout the paper, p
always denotes a sufficiently large prime number and χ denotes an
non-principal multiplicative character modulo p. We assume that Fp is
represented by the set {0, . . . , p − 1}.
  Furthermore, G always denotes a multiplicative subgroup of F∗p of
order #G = T and I always denotes the set I = {1, . . . , H}.
  We also assume that f ∈ Fp [X] is a of degree d ≥ 1. In particular,
f is not a constant.

inequality |U| ≤ c V with some constant c > 0 that may depend on the
real parameter ε > 0 and the integer parameters d ≥ 1 and ν ≥ 1 and

   In particular, all our estimates are uniform with respect to the poly-
nomial f and the character χ.

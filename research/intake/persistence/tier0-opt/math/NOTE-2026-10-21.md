## 0. Setting and notation

**0.1 The enumeration problem.** Let S = {2^a 3^b 7^c : a, b, c ≥ 0} and let p(y) be the product of the decimal digits
of y. For a vertex s ∈ S of an antecedent tree, the *children* of s are the y ∈ S with y ≥ 10 and p(y) = s [PAPER §1].
For the targets of interest the digits 0 and 5 cannot occur in a child (a 0 makes p(y) = 0; a 5 puts a prime 5 in
p(y) ∈ S). Let M be the multiset of the q digits of y that differ from 1 (digits in {2,3,4,6,7,8,9}); then p(y) is the
product of M, and q = |M| is the quantity called k in the Baker material (§4).

**0.2 The equation.** A child with digit multiset M is a solution of

    2^a 3^b 7^c = y = R_n + Σ_{j=1..q} (d_j − 1) 10^{e_j},   R_n = (10^n − 1)/9,   n > e_1 > … > e_q ≥ 0.        (E)

Equivalently 9y + 1 = 10^n + Σ_j c_j 10^{e_j} with c_j = 9(d_j − 1) ∈ {9, 18, …, 72}.

**0.3 Patterns.** For a level L, a *pattern* is (n mod L, multiset of pairs (d, e mod L)). For a prime p ∤ 10 dividing
10^L − 1, y mod p depends only on the pattern. The levels used are L = 12, 24, 48, 96. The sieve at level L tests, for
each pattern value V and each a, whether V lies in 2^a·⟨3,7⟩ modulo the product of the new primes; a pattern that
passes has an exponent coset in (b, c) [RESULT, Method steps 2–4].

**0.4 Level 96.** The 20 primes of 10^96 − 1 outside {2,3,5,7} are 11, 13, 37, 101, 9901, 73, 137, 99990001, 17,
5882353, 9999999900000001, 97, 353, 449, 641, 1409, 69857, 206209, 66554101249, 75118313082913 [RESULT, Method step 2].
Put Q = their product.

*(drafter)* Recomputed exactly: R_96 = (10^96 − 1)/9 equals 21·Q, with Q = 5.291×10^93; the 20 listed factors are
distinct. (Primality of the large factors was not rechecked.) This is the identity "R_96 = 21Q" of [H1] §1.

Let Λ_96 = {(b, c) ∈ Z² : 3^b 7^c ≡ 1 (mod p) for the 20 primes}. Its index in Z² is 2.80999…×10^56 and its shortest
nonzero vector has Euclidean length 7.85×10^27 [DEEPCUT; H1 §1 A3; reproduced independently by a blind
re-implementation, DEEPCUT "Blind re-implementation"]. *(drafter)* The basis recorded in [CDA, Theorem 3],
u = (−5834300434592217394015440000, −5252227908597256497593760000) and v = (24550795595028192589139040000,
−26061931100684490688131840000), has |det| = 280999509749509611991995562563818636457519792000000000000, which equals the
index printed by the H1 lane's `counterexample.out`. That equality shows ⟨u, v⟩ = Λ_96 provided u and v lie in Λ_96;
membership (20 congruences each) was not rechecked here.

**0.5 Anchors.** For a pattern with value V = R_ν + Σ(d_j − 1)10^{e_j} (positions e_j ∈ [0, 96), ν ∈ [0, 96)), an
*anchor* is an S-unit y with 0 < y < R_96 and an exact identity y + k·R_96 = V, k ∈ Z [H1 §1]. A pattern is *anchored*
if it has an anchor.

**0.6 Two senses of "open".** *(drafter)* [H1] §5 defines Open96(V, a) as ∃ b, c : 2^a 3^b 7^c ≡ V (mod Q), the
congruence condition only. The engine's OPEN verdict additionally applies a tail test (the lowest K ≤ 5 digits) and a
2-adic descent, which can only remove patterns. The sources use "open" for both; this note writes *sieve-open* for the
first and *engine-open* for the second when the difference matters.

## 1. The open set at level 96: anchored patterns plus accidents

### 1.1 The claim and its status

[HYP] H1 (the Anchoring Lemma) claimed that every pattern open at level 96 is anchored by an S-unit of explicit small
height. Status in [HYP]: **KILLED as stated** (2026-10-20, [H1]). The surviving statement is the restricted form tagged
**CONJECTURE** (§1.7). The structure of the open set is tagged **HEURISTIC** ([H1] A4) and its anchored part is
tagged PROVEN-ON-PAPER ([H1] A2).

### 1.2 An exact identity carries a congruence and nothing else

**Proposition 1.1 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER ([H1] A1; Lean-ready
statements `exact_identity_iff` and `k_bound` in [H1] §5, not machine-checked).
Let V = R_ν + Σ_{j≤q}(d_j − 1)10^{e_j} with ν, e_j ∈ [0, 96) and d_j ∈ [2, 9]. For 0 < y < R_96 there is an integer k
with y + k·R_96 = V if and only if y ≡ V (mod R_96), that is, y is the least positive residue of V; and then
0 ≤ k < 1 + 8q.

*Proof.* The first assertion is the definition of congruence. For the range of k: V ≥ 1 and y < R_96 give
k = (V − y)/R_96 > (1 − R_96)/R_96 > −1, so k ≥ 0. Since 10^95 ≤ R_96, each term (d_j − 1)10^{e_j} is at most
8·R_96 and R_ν < R_96, so V < (1 + 8q)R_96. ∎ *(drafter, tightening the source's k ≤ 1 + 8q.)*

*Seam (as the source states it).* The sieve tests the congruence modulo Q only, and R_96 = 21Q; the 21-part of the
congruence is not tested by the sieve. For 3126 of the 3154 recorded (vertex, multiset, anchor) triples the sweep
stores (a, y) but not (ν, ε); the 21-part is checked only for the 27 rows with recorded positions (27/27 identities,
all k = 0) and, for the rest, only that some representation of y with the multiset M exists (`run_exact`) [H1 §2].

**Proposition 1.2 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER ([H1] A2; the congruence
core is the Lean lemma `candidate_modEq`).
If a pattern has an anchor y = 2^a 3^{b_y} 7^{c_y} then it is sieve-open: (b_y, c_y) satisfies the congruences of the
20 primes at the exponent a, and every integer realizing the pattern is ≡ y (mod R_96).

*Proof.* y ≡ V (mod R_96) gives y ≡ V (mod Q), so (b_y, c_y) lies in the exponent coset. An integer realizing the
pattern differs from V by a multiple of 10^96 − 1 (shifting positions by multiples of 96 changes each 10^{e} by a
multiple of 10^96 − 1, and R_n changes likewise), hence is ≡ V mod R_96. ∎

*Reading note (drafter).* Proposition 1.2 gives sieve-openness. The sources do not claim that an anchored pattern
survives the tail test and descent, and Lemma C (§3) closes most anchored patterns (DEEPCUT: 288 of 299 at depth ≤ 2).
Of the 299 patterns that remain engine-open after the sweep, [PAPER] §5 and [H1] §2 report that every one is anchored.

### 1.3 No counting argument forces a small anchor

**Proposition 1.3 (proved on paper; not machine-checked, with a computed input).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER
([H1] A3).
(i) A box [0, T]² of exponent pairs meets every one of the I = [Z² : Λ_96] cosets of Λ_96 only if (T + 1)² ≥ I,
that is T ≥ 1.67×10^28 for I = 2.81×10^56. (ii) The S-units below 10^98 with a fixed 2-adic exponent number about
1.2×10^4, covering at most a fraction 4×10^−53 of the cosets.

*Proof.* (i) The box contains (T + 1)² lattice points, each in one coset. (ii) The pairs (b, c) ≥ 0 with
b ln 3 + c ln 7 ≤ 98 ln 10 form a triangle of area (98 ln 10)²/(2 ln 3 ln 7) ≈ 1.19×10^4. ∎ *(drafter: both
arithmetic steps recomputed by hand.)* The computed input is I and the shortest vector of Λ_96 (§0.4).

Consequence stated in the source: a small coset point is never forced; for a pattern not built from a small S-unit it
is a coincidence of probability about 10^−52.

### 1.4 The accident model

**Heuristic 1.4 (HEURISTIC; [H1] A4).** Let H be the image of (b, c) ↦ 3^b 7^c in (Z/Q)^*; |H| = I = 2.81×10^56. The
sieve-open set at level 96 is modeled as the union of (i) anchored patterns, sieve-open with certainty by
Proposition 1.2, and (ii) *accidents*: patterns whose value lands in 2^a·H modulo Q by chance. If pattern values were
uniform modulo Q, an accident has probability ρ = |H|/Q = 5.3×10^−38 per (pattern, a). The expected number of accidents
for an equation is μ = N_96·ρ with N_96 = 96·Π_d C(95 + m_d, m_d), m_d the multiplicity of digit d. An accident's
nearest coset point lies near 10^27 or beyond (Proposition 1.3), so it never has a small anchor.

*Seam on ρ.* [H1] records that its first table used |H|/φ(Q) = 7.3×10^−38, 1.37 times too high, and that the correct
figure is |H|/Q. Its table of μ values (q = 24: 1.4×10^−5; q = 30: 2.3; q = 36: 1.5×10^5; q = 40: 2.2×10^8; q = 44:
1.35×10^11, for the shape two thirds twos and one third threes) is annotated "times 0.73 for |H|/Q"; [HYP] quotes the
table without that factor.

### 1.5 The constructed counterexample

**Proposition 1.5 (proved on paper, with exact computation; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER +
exact computation ([H1] §4; [HYP] H1).
Let u = 3^156·7^214 (256 decimal digits), R = R_96, V = u mod R (95 digits), ν = 0, and let the pattern P* be read from
the decimal digits of V: each nonzero digit z ≤ 8 at position e gives an entry (z + 1, e), and each digit 9 gives the
two entries (9, e) and (2, e). Then:

1. V = Σ_j (d_j − 1)10^{e_j} exactly, with q = 100 entries, and P* passes the congruences of all 20 primes with the
   exponent coset (156, 214) + Λ_96 at a = 0.
2. The engine's `classify` returns OPEN with K = 5 and `descend` returns ('OPEN', set()) for P* (exact computation by
   the lane's code; not rerun here).
3. P* has no anchor at any a. In particular no S-unit y < 10^98 lies in its coset at a = 0.

*Proof.* (1) By construction V ≡ u (mod R), hence modulo each of the 20 primes, and u = 3^156 7^214 has exponents
(156, 214) at a = 0. (3) Suppose y is an anchor. Both y and V lie in [0, R), so Proposition 1.1 gives y = V.
At a = 0: if y ≡ u (mod Q) is an S-unit with exponents (b', c'), then (b' − 156, c' − 214) ∈ Λ_96; it is nonzero or
y = u; y = u is excluded because u has 256 > 96 digits; a nonzero vector has length ≥ 7.85×10^27, so
max(b', c') > 5×10^27 and y has more than 10^27 digits, contradicting y < 10^96. ∎

*Seam in the source, closed by the drafter.* The source argument (and `counterexample.py`, which stops once a = 0
yields hits) addresses a = 0. For a' ≥ 1 an anchor would require 2^{a'} ∈ ⟨3, 7⟩ modulo Q, which the report does not
rule out. *(drafter)* Because y = V is forced (Proposition 1.1), the question reduces to whether the 95-digit integer V
is itself a {2,3,7}-unit. Recomputed exactly: V has 95 digits, 86 nonzero digits of which 14 are 9s (so q = 86 + 14 =
100, as the source states), and after removing all factors 2, 3, 7 from V a 94-digit cofactor ≠ 1 remains. Hence P* has
no anchor at any a. This recomputation is a one-time check by the drafter, not a Cairn gate.

*Caveats the source states.* q = 100 lies far outside any program vertex. *(drafter)* The printed multiset of the first
counterexample also contains nine 5s, so P* is not a pattern of any equation in the program's digit alphabet (§0.1).
The construction refutes the unconditional statement and exhibits the mechanism; it is not a Tier-C counterexample.
The first run, with exponents in [40, 240], produced u with 55 digits (< R_96), which are anchored, not
counterexamples; the report records catching this from `u_digits` and rerunning. Search: exponents B, C ∈ [150, 260),
a ∈ {0..3}, tail test only, stopped after 477 hits at a = 0.

## 2. The branch-split refinement and why it is exact

### 2.1 Statement

Let a pattern at level 12 (n ≡ ν mod 12; digits d_j at classes e_j mod 12) and let y' be any integer realizing it with
y' = 2^a 3^b 7^c. [IDEAS] R1.1 / [HYP] H11: the pattern determines whether 3 | y' and whether 7 | y', hence whether
b = 0 and whether c = 0; the sieve may therefore test membership in ⟨2,3,7⟩, ⟨2,3⟩, ⟨2,7⟩ or ⟨2⟩ according to the
branch, instead of always in ⟨2,3,7⟩.

### 2.2 Soundness

**Proposition 2.1 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER ([R1] "Soundness"; the lane's
"gcd test" description).
(i) y' mod 3 = (n + Σ_j (d_j − 1)) mod 3, a function of ν mod 3 and the multiset. (ii) y' mod 7 is a function of ν mod 6
and the classes e_j mod 6. (iii) 3 ∤ y' forces b = 0 and 7 ∤ y' forces c = 0. (iv) Consequently, if a pattern's
exponent coset (for the given a) contains no point with b = 0 when 3 ∤ V, or none with c = 0 when 7 ∤ V, then no
integer realizing the pattern is a child, and the pattern may be dropped; the filter never edits a coset.

*Proof.* (i) 10 ≡ 1 (mod 3), so 10^e ≡ 1 and R_n ≡ n. (ii) ord_7(10) = 6, which divides 12; 9 is invertible modulo 7, so
R_n = (10^n − 1)/9 mod 7 depends on n mod 6, and each 10^{e_j} depends on e_j mod 6. (iii) b ≥ 1 iff 3 | y', c ≥ 1 iff
7 | y'. (iv) A true child has (b, c) in the coset with the forced zeros, so a coset with no such point carries no child.
Since the test reads only residue data of the level-12 pattern, any integer realizing it gives the same verdict. ∎

**Proposition 2.2 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER ([R1]; [COORD] chat
23:05: "children inherit the branch").
A child at level 2L of a level-L pattern has the same y' mod 3 and y' mod 7 as its parent, so the branch is inherited
and a true child passes the filter at every ancestor level.

*Proof.* The residues of y' modulo 3 and 7 are functions of the level-12 data (Proposition 2.1), and a level-2L pattern
reduces to its level-L parent by taking residues mod L, so both describe the same integers. ∎

*Other points from the source.* y' = 3 or 7 is excluded by y' ≥ 10; a = 0 is harmless; EXACT (fully determined) lines
are unfiltered. The mod-27 difficulty raised in IDEAS.md (not read) "does not arise" ([R1]).

### 2.3 In what sense it is exact

*(drafter)* Two statements must be kept apart.

1. **Exact for the verdict (Proposition 2.1 (iv)).** The filter drops only patterns that carry no child, so the set of
   children found for any equation is unchanged. This is the PROVEN-ON-PAPER statement. Evidence: 87/87 equations
   (vertices 72, 224, 10800, 14336, 8192, 28672) return identical solutions and children; 608/608 planted children are
   recovered in all four (b > 0, c > 0) classes [R1 gates (a), (b)].
2. **Unchanged open set (empirical only).** [R1] gate (c) reports final level-96 open sets identical to production in all
   87 equations, with every removed pattern (1,042,583 for 14336, 8192, 10800 and in full for 72 and 224; a 1/40 sample,
   35,917, for 28672) re-checked infeasible by an independent congruence solve. This is not a theorem. *(drafter)* A
   proof of equality would need every engine-open pattern to satisfy y ≡ V (mod 21) at its anchor; Proposition 1.1 shows
   the sieve does not test that, so an open pattern anchored by some y with y ≢ V (mod 3) or (mod 7) would be dropped by
   the split though open in production. No source exhibits one, and the 27 positioned rows of [H1] §2 all have k = 0.

The explanation [R1] offers for identical open sets, "later survivors are already branch-compatible", is argued, not
tested.

## 3. The depth-1 Lemma C automaton and its equivalence

### 3.1 Lemma C at depth 1

[DEEPCUT] (tag STRONG-EMPIRICAL: three implementations in two model families, with Lean for `candidate_modEq` and
`deep_cut_core`; the enumeration and lattice steps are not machine-checked).

Let P be an open level-96 pattern of an equation (s, M), with 2-adic exponent a, positions e_j ∈ [0, 96) and exponent
coset C ⊂ Z² in (b, c). Suppose every point of C other than one explicitly checked point has max(b, c) large enough
that the candidate has at least 192 digits (true here: the shortest vector of Λ_96 gives about 10^27 digits). Then every
other candidate y' = 2^a 3^b 7^c, (b, c) ∈ C, satisfies, at depth D = 1,

    y' ≡ L := R_96 + Σ_{j∈S} (d_j − 1) 10^{e_j}   (mod 10^96),

where S ranges over the sets of pattern digits placed at copy 0 (physical positions e_j < 96), at most one per residue
class (two digits cannot share a physical position); digits not in S sit at positions ≥ 96 and do not affect the lowest
96 digits, and R_n ≡ R_96 (mod 10^96) because n ≥ 192. Then v_2(L) = a and 3^b 7^c ≡ L/2^a modulo 5^96 and modulo
2^{96−a}: three linear congruences in (b, c) (`cut_coset`). If for every admissible S these are inconsistent with C,
the pattern is CLOSED apart from the checked point.

### 3.2 No carries at depth 1

**Proposition 3.1 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER ([H2M] Step 1).
Every depth-1 value L_S is the digit string (l_0, …, l_95) with l_e = d if class e contributes the digit d and l_e = 1
otherwise, no carries occur, and the number of cases S yielding a given string is Π over positions with l_e ≠ 1 of
m(e, l_e), where m(e, d) is the number of pattern digits equal to d in class e.

*Proof.* At most one digit per class is chosen, so the digit at e is 1 + (d − 1) = d ∈ [2, 9] or 1 (digits of M are at
least 2); every digit lies in 0..9 and L_S = Σ_e l_e 10^e. A string arises from the choice of one of the m(e, l_e)
equal digits at each non-1 position. ∎

**Proposition 3.2 (proved on paper; not machine-checked, with a computed input).** Source tag: PROVED ON PAPER

### 3.4 The cut is a congruence

**Proposition 3.3 (proved on paper; not machine-checked; conditional on Fact F).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER
conditional on Fact F, which [H2M] and [HYP] tag STRONG-EMPIRICAL ([H2M] Step 2; [HYP] H2).
Let y = 2^a 3^b 7^c be an anchor with a ≤ 87 and C_y = (b, c) + Λ_96. For an integer L the cut congruences
y' = 2^a 3^{b'} 7^{c'} ≡ L (mod 10^96) with (b', c') ∈ C_y are solvable if and only if

    L ≡ y   (mod 5^5 · 2^{a+9}).                                                                        (Acc)

This implies v_2(L) = a and 5 ∤ L.

*Proof.* Write (b', c') = (b, c) + λ with λ ∈ Λ_96 and g = 3^{λ_1}7^{λ_2}, so y' = y·g. For the 5-part: y g ≡ L
(mod 5^96) iff L y^{−1} ≡ g, so L y^{−1} (mod 5^96) must lie in the image of Λ_96. For the 2-part: y g ≡ L (mod 2^96) with
v_2(y) = a < 96 is equivalent to (y/2^a) g ≡ L/2^a (mod 2^{96−a}) when v_2(L) = a, and impossible otherwise. By Fact F
the element (L y^{−1} mod 5^96, (L/2^a)(y/2^a)^{−1} mod 2^{96−a}) lies in the image iff it is ≡ 1 modulo 5^5 and modulo
2^9, i.e. L ≡ y (mod 5^5) and L/2^a ≡ y/2^a (mod 2^9), i.e. (Acc). Finally (Acc) gives v_2(L) = v_2(y) = a (since
a + 9 > a) and 5 ∤ L (since 5 ∤ y). ∎

### 3.5 The automaton

**Proposition 3.4 (proved on paper; not machine-checked; conditional on Fact F).** Source tag: PROVEN-ON-PAPER
conditional on Fact F ([H2M] Step 3 and "Equivalence claim").
Put w = a + 9, so 5^5·2^{a+9} divides 10^w. Then (Acc) depends only on the digits l_0, …, l_{w−1} of L_S, and holds iff
the sequence q_e = y − Σ_{i<e} l_i 10^i satisfies q_w ≡ 0 (mod 2^w) and q_5 ≡ 0 (mod 5^5). The 2-adic condition is
enforced digit by digit: given q_e ≡ 0 (mod 2^e), q_{e+1} ≡ 0 (mod 2^{e+1}) iff l_e ≡ q_e/2^e (mod 2). The 5-adic
condition reads only l_0, …, l_4. Thus the automaton A_y, with state (q_e mod 2^w, and q_e mod 5^5 while e < 5), has at
most five outgoing digits per state, and depends only on (y, a): the pattern enters only through its masks D_e (the
digits it holds in class e) for e < w.

*Proof.* The first sentence is the divisibility 5^5 2^{a+9} | 10^w for w ≥ 5. For the digit recursion: if
y ≡ Σ_{i<w} l_i 10^i (mod 2^w) then for every e ≤ w the same holds modulo 2^e, because the terms with i ≥ e are divisible
by 2^e; conversely the recursion gives q_w ≡ 0 (mod 2^w). The parity step uses q_{e+1} = q_e − l_e 10^e = 2^e (q_e/2^e −
l_e 5^e) and 5^e odd. Of the digits 1..9 five are odd and four even. For e ≥ 5, 10^e ≡ 0 (mod 5^5). ∎

**Per-anchor closure.** For one anchor, compile the lazily memoized transition table of A_y (and, for small a, the list
W_y of all accepting strings recorded by their non-1 entries). A pattern representation is *open* iff some accepting
string has all its entries inside the pattern's low profile {(e, d) : e < w, d ∈ D_e}. The number of surviving depth-1
cases is the sum over contained witnesses of the product of multiplicities (Proposition 3.1), times Π_{e ≥ w}(1 + n_e),
where n_e is the number of pattern digits in class e (those digits sit at copy 0 or not without affecting (Acc)). The
verdict for a pattern is the conjunction over its representations. A signature cache keyed on the low profile shares
work, and sharing keys on the coset residue y mod 5^5·2^{a+9}, not on the anchor [H2M, H2R].

*Equivalence claim ([H2M]).* For every anchor and representation, the per-anchor count equals `len(stats[0])` of
`deepcut.deep_cut(C_y, a, rep, 1)` and the verdicts agree. Argument: Proposition 3.1 gives a bijection between
deepcut's depth-1 cases and (string, choice of digits) pairs; Proposition 3.3 replaces the per-case test.

## 4. The two-ended Baker chain with 2^a folded in, and its bound table

### 4.1 Setting

Let y' = R_n + Σ_{j=1..k} (d_j − 1) 10^{E_j}, d_j ∈ {2,…,9}, n > E_1 > … > E_k ≥ 0, y' = 2^a 3^b 7^c. Put M = 9y',
c_j = 9(d_j − 1), g_0 = n − E_1, g_j = E_j − E_{j+1}. Partial sums
N_j = 10^{n−E_j} + Σ_{i≤j} c_i 10^{E_i−E_j} and T_j = Σ_{i>j} c_i 10^{E_i} − 1 (T_k = −1) satisfy
M + 1 = N_j 10^{E_j} + Σ_{i>j} c_i 10^{E_i}, hence M − T_j = N_j 10^{E_j} (N_0 = 1, E_0 = n) and
10^{n−E_j} ≤ N_j ≤ 9·10^{n−E_j} [BAKER §1].

### 4.2 The top-down (archimedean) chain

Matveev's theorem in the form of Bugeaud–Mignotte–Siksek, Thm 9.4 (hypothesis (A1) below): for real algebraic numbers
in Q, Λ = α_1^{b_1}⋯α_t^{b_t} − 1 ≠ 0, log|Λ| > −C(t)(1 + log B)A_1⋯A_t with C(t) = 1.4·30^{t+3} t^{4.5},
A_i ≥ max(|log α_i|, h(α_i), 0.16), B ≥ max|b_i| [BAKER §2].

**Lemma 4.1 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: ordinary proof ([BAKER] §3, "not machine-checked").
With Λ_0 = M·10^{−n} − 1 and Λ_j = M·10^{−E_j}·N_j^{−1} − 1: for k ≥ 1, 0 < Λ_0 ≤ 80·10^{−g_0}; for 1 ≤ j ≤ k − 1,
0 < Λ_j ≤ 80·10^{−g_j}/N_j; and Λ_k = −1/(M + 1).

*Proof.* Λ_j = (Σ_{i>j} c_i 10^{E_i} − 1)/(N_j 10^{E_j}). Since c_i ≤ 72 and the positions are distinct, the numerator is
at most 72·10^{E_{j+1}}(1 + 10^−1 + …) ≤ 80·10^{E_{j+1}} and, for j < k, at least 9·10^{E_{j+1}} − 1 ≥ 8 > 0. For j = k
the numerator is −1 and N_k 10^{E_k} = M + 1. ∎

**Proposition 4.2 (proved on paper; not machine-checked; conditional on (A1)).** Source tag: [BAKER] "ordinary proof"
conditional on Matveev's theorem; reviewed by Codex ("sound after fixes, high confidence; all 99 tabulated bounds
valid", [SANDWICH] "Independent review").
Let t_0 = 3, P_0 = log 3·log 7·log 10 if a = 0, and t_0 = 4, P_0 = log 2·log 3·log 7·log 10 if a ≥ 1; B_max(n) =

### 4.3 The bottom-up (5-adic) chain

### 4.5 Limits of the two-ended scheme

* **Optimality in its class ([B3], PROVEN-ON-PAPER as stated, LP exact on k = 6, 10, 14).** If an interior gap is
  bounded only by the prefix above it (archimedean) or the suffix below it (5-adic), the maximum of the admissible
  length equals min_m [K_0(1 + K)^{m−1} + Z_0(1 + Y)^{k−m}], so the meet in the middle is optimal in that class,
  about 8.7 digits per gap. [B1] B3 states the structural consequence: the exponent cannot drop below (k/2) log10 λ.
* **Negative results ([B3]).** Hypergeometric, Padé and Thue–Siegel routes: HEURISTIC, Padé denominators not built;
  Bennett 1997 (Thm 1.1, Cor 1.2) gives an effective measure for one fixed (A/B)^{1/m} with λ ≥ 2.2, which on the top
  gap gives g_0 ≤ λn/m, linear in n. p-adic information at 3 or 7 gives n ≲ C·E_{j+1}·s_j, vacuous. Bugeaud–Laurent
  two-logarithm bounds are inapplicable: the 5-adic forms carry 3 logarithms. End reduction (Baker–Davenport/LLL) gains
  about 10^12 combined (top gap 10^14.7 → about 10^3); each further enumerated level trades 10^14 for about 10^4 nodes
  (CONJECTURE, lattice bounds not computed).
* **The range of a.** The fold is certified for a ≤ 40 and the table for a = 1 and a = 40 agree to the digits printed.
  [B1] §1.1 and [COORD] (chat 18:55) report that the engine's 2-adic bound `two_adic_bound` does not terminate for
  multisets with k ≥ 36 and that the true A for Tier-C multisets is at least 100–160. The table of this section
  therefore does not cover those a; [B1] §6 states it did not recompute G with a bounded by A(M).
* **Disagreement on the Codex numbers.** [B1] scales Codex's three reported points linearly in k and records them as
  "reported, not re-derived".

## 5. The linear block lift, the separation certificate, and two impossibility results

All of §5 is from [CDA] (Codex, blind to [B1]–[B3]). The source tags every result "PROVED ON PAPER. Confidence HIGH" and
states that nothing has Cairn's PROVEN status. Verdict of the source: "The barrier is not broken."

**Setting.** Fix a ≥ 0, a parent exponent coset C = p + Zu + Zv (a ≥ 0 fixed, exponents (b, c) integers) all of whose
S-units 2^a 3^b 7^c agree modulo 10^s. Let y_0 be the S-unit at p, g_u = 3^{u_1}7^{u_2}, g_v = 3^{v_1}7^{v_2} (negative
exponents mean modular inverses). Agreement modulo 10^s means v_2(y_0(g − 1)) ≥ s and v_5(g − 1) ≥ s, that is
v_2(g − 1) ≥ s − a, for g ∈ {g_u, g_v}.

### 5.1 The exact linear lift

**Proposition 5.1 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVED ON PAPER, Confidence HIGH ([CDA]
Theorem 1); 3,528 direct comparisons.
Let 1 ≤ h ≤ s − a and M_h = 10^h. Put A_u = y_0(g_u − 1)/10^s and A_v = y_0(g_v − 1)/10^s modulo M_h (integral). Let T be
the target suffix of length s + h with the new block filled by 1s and the previously fixed digits kept, let
β = (T − y_0)/10^s, and let the optional extra digits d_j sit at positions s + e_j (0 ≤ e_j < h) with
w_j = (d_j − 1)10^{e_j}. Then extending the suffix by h digits is equivalent to

    A_u z_1 + A_v z_2 − Σ_j w_j x_j ≡ β (mod 10^h),   x_j ∈ {0, 1},

where (z_1, z_2) ∈ Z² indexes the exponent point p + z_1 u + z_2 v, subject to the multiplicity constraints and at most
one chosen digit per physical position.

*Proof (drafter's expansion of the source's proof).* Write g = 1 + t. Then v_2(t) ≥ s − a and v_5(t) ≥ s. Since
v_2(y_0) = a and 5 ∤ y_0, every term of y_0·t² has 2-adic valuation ≥ a + 2(s − a) = 2s − a ≥ s + h (because h ≤ s − a)
and 5-adic valuation ≥ 2s ≥ s + h. So y_0 t² ≡ 0 (mod 10^{s+h}), and likewise y_0 t_u t_v and every term with total degree
≥ 2. Hence for integers z ≥ 0, y_0 g^z ≡ y_0 + z y_0 t (mod 10^{s+h}), and y_0 g_u^{z_1} g_v^{z_2} ≡
y_0 + z_1 y_0 t_u + z_2 y_0 t_v. For negative z, (1 + t)^{−1} = 1 − t + t²(1 + t)^{−1}, and (1 + t) is a unit modulo
10^{s+h}, so the same linearization holds. Because 10^s divides y_0 t_u and y_0 t_v, the target y_0 g_u^{z_1} g_v^{z_2} ≡
T + 10^s Σ_j w_j x_j (mod 10^{s+h}), with T ≡ y_0 (mod 10^s), divides by 10^s to the stated congruence. Construction is
polynomial in the precision, the number of digit variables, and the bit length of the basis. ∎

*Checks reported.* a = 0..3, s = a + 3..a + 6, every permitted h, z_1, z_2 ∈ [−3, 3]: all passed; over-precision fails as
predicted (558860141 against the linear prediction 158860141). *(drafter)* The source states the hypothesis s ≥ a + 3;
the proof above uses only h ≤ s − a.

### 5.2 The separation certificate

**Proposition 5.2 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVED ON PAPER, Confidence HIGH ([CDA]
Theorem 2); 510 small tests: 180 sound rejections, all 318 feasible instances survived, twelve infeasible instances
undecided.
Suppose |z_i| ≤ Z_i. For an integer multiplier μ let r_i = [μA_i]_{M_h} and t_j = [μ w_j]_{M_h} be centered residues,
D = Z_1|r_1| + Z_2|r_2|, L = −D − Σ_j max(t_j, 0), U = D + Σ_j max(−t_j, 0), and b = μβ mod M_h. If
⌈(L − b)/M_h⌉ > ⌊(U − b)/M_h⌋, no choice of (z_1, z_2, x) in the box satisfies the congruence of Proposition 5.1.

*Proof.* The integer r_1 z_1 + r_2 z_2 − Σ t_j x_j lies in [L, U] and is ≡ b (mod M_h) for any solution, so [L, U] would
contain b + mM_h for some integer m. The displayed inequality says it contains none. Dropping collision constraints only
enlarges the interval. ∎

Verification is polynomial in the data without listing copy choices. The source states that finding a successful μ has
no established subexponential guarantee.

**Failure regime (proved on paper; not machine-checked; [CDA] "Where this breaks").** If Z_1, Z_2 ≥ M_h and
gcd(A_u, A_v, M_h) = 1, every μ fails.

*Proof.* If r_1 or r_2 is nonzero, D ≥ M_h and U − L ≥ 2D ≥ 2M_h, so [L − b, U − b] contains a multiple of M_h. If both
vanish, μA_u ≡ μA_v ≡ 0 and the gcd hypothesis forces μ ≡ 0 (mod M_h), whence every t_j and b vanish and [L, U] = {0}
contains 0. ∎ *(drafter, tightening the source's two-sentence proof.)* All 9,900 small wide-box tests were undecided. On
the reachable Tier-C parent of §5.5, the box 0 ≤ b, c ≤ 10^600 gives coordinate bounds of 528 and 527 decimal digits
against M_h = 10^96, and the coefficient gcd is 1: the obstruction applies exactly.

### 5.3 Impossibility 1: an explicit output list holds 2^r cosets

**Proposition 5.3 (proved on paper; not machine-checked, with a computed input).** Source tag: PROVED ON PAPER,
Confidence HIGH ([CDA] Theorem 3; the saturation half is Proposition 3.2 of this note).
Assume K − a ≥ 9 and K ≥ 5, so the image of Λ_96 in G_K is exactly (1 + 2^9Z)/2^{K−a} × (1 + 5^5Z)/5^K. Let t_0 be a
suffix of length s ≥ a + 9 attained by a point of the parent coset, and let d_1, …, d_r be pattern digits in r distinct
classes at positions e_i ∈ [s, K). For X ⊆ {1..r} let t_X be the K-digit target equal to t_0 below position s, with 1s
at positions in [s, K) except the digits d_i, i ∈ X. Then the 2^r targets t_X have distinct, pairwise disjoint, nonempty
exponent fibers inside the parent; consequently any explicit list of the sub-cosets compatible with the pattern's
extensions contains at least 2^r cosets.

*Proof.* Distinct targets are distinct residues, so their fibers are disjoint. Let y_1 be a point of the parent with
suffix t_0. Then t_X/y_1 is ≡ 1 modulo 5^s and modulo 2^{s−a} (after dividing the 2-parts by 2^a), and since s ≥ a + 9
and s ≥ 5 it lies in the principal-unit group above, which is the image of Λ_96; so the fiber is nonempty. ∎
*(drafter: the source states the result for "r independent occupied residue classes with one available digit each" and
"once a suffix of length s ≥ a + 9 is feasible"; the formulation above is the drafter's reading, with "feasible" taken to
mean attained by a point of the parent coset.)*

For repeated digits the one-block count is Π_e (1 + t_e), where t_e is the number of distinct available digit values in
class e; labeling identical copies separately overcounts. Checks: image orders at K = 9, 10, 11, a = 0 were 625, 6250,
62500; all 510 targets from binary families with r = 1..8 were distinct and compatible.

This result is the paper form of the cost correction of [SANDWICH]: every extension of a depth-1 survivor survives, so
the number of sub-cosets at depth D is about (D + 1)^k.

### 5.4 Impossibility 2: one optional digit 2 destroys affine-hull compression

**Proposition 5.4 (proved on paper; not machine-checked).** Source tag: PROVED ON PAPER, Confidence HIGH ([CDA]
Theorem 4); 28 pairs 9 ≤ m < K ≤ 16 had the predicted order.
Under the saturation hypotheses of Proposition 5.3, let m ≥ a + 9 and K > m. Let two targets share all positions below m
and differ only by an optional digit 2 at position m. Then the smallest affine lattice of exponent pairs containing both
unbounded fibers at precision K is exactly the exponent coset determined by the suffix modulo 10^m.

*Proof.* The ratio ρ = t_2/t_1 = 1 + 10^m/t_1 has v_2(ρ − 1) = m − a and v_5(ρ − 1) = m exactly. Elements of exact
valuation m − a (resp. m) generate the cyclic 2-part (1 + 2^{m−a}Z)/2^{K−a} of order 2^{K−m} (resp. the 5-part of order
5^{K−m}); by coprimality ρ generates the whole kernel of reduction from precision K to precision m, of order 10^{K−m}.
The fibers are cosets of the kernel lattice Λ^{(K)} of the exponent map φ_K, and the affine hull of two of them is
q_1 + Λ^{(K)} + Z(q_2 − q_1) = φ_K^{−1}(t_1⟨ρ⟩) = φ_K^{−1}(t_1·ker(K → m)), which is the precision-m fiber. All targets
share the suffix mod 10^m, so they all lie in it, and the hull equals it. ∎

*(drafter: the source's proof is two sentences; the hull as a preimage is made explicit.)* Consequence: compressing a
family that contains an optional 2 at position m to one enclosing lattice discards the information at positions ≥ m,
admitting unwanted digits (check: m = 9, K = 12, two target words generate a hull with 1,000 suffixes, including an
unwanted digit 3).

## 6. The obstruction map: why every current method is exponential in k, and the precise targets

### 6.1 Three multiplying factors ([B1] §0)

1. **Baker bound exponential in k.** With S_j the prefix length, S_{j+1} ≤ (1 + λ)S_j where λ is the per-gap
   constant. The certified table gives about 10^{18–19} per non-1 digit; the one-ended bound for the k = 44 vertex is
   log10 G = 849.7.
2. **Lattice barrier.** A rank-2 lattice of index det certifies n above about √det only (§6.2 M2); with the 10-adic
   window det ≈ 10^K, so beating 10^850 needs K ≈ 1700 pinned low digits, depth D = 18 one-ended and 7–10 two-ended.
3. **Copy choices.** Each pinned window of depth D multiplies the work by (D + 1)^q (the number of ways the q digits sit
   inside or outside the window); at k = 44 this is 10^56 per pattern (10^46 two-ended), on top of 10^{19–22}
   level-12 patterns and 10^{9–12} depth-1 survivors per vertex.

the two ends (top through the archimedean form, bottom through the 10-adic window or the 5-adic form), paying a
multiplicative cost per digit crossed.

### 6.2 Propositions on the cost floors ([B1] §2)

**M1 (PROVEN-ON-PAPER; congruence core is Lean `candidate_modEq`).** For every L and every S-unit y < R_L with a
representation as a pattern of the equation, that pattern passes every congruence modulo the primes of 10^L − 1.
Hence the open set at every level contains the anchored patterns, a purely modular ladder never reaches the empty set,
and an archimedean upper bound is logically required; the ladder only raises the lower bound. (This is Proposition 1.2.)

**M2, the √det barrier (PROVEN-ON-PAPER arithmetic of orders).** For a rank-2 lattice Λ of index det with Lagrange–Gauss
reduced basis, λ_1λ_2 ≤ (2/√3)det and the covering radius is at most λ_2/√2, so a coset's nearest point lies within
about √det in the worst case, and a modulus of index det certifies n above about √det and no more. For Λ_96,
√det = 5.3×10^28 against the measured shortest vector 7.85×10^27 (balanced, STRONG-EMPIRICAL). Certifying n > G needs
det ≥ about G². The 10-adic window gives det ≈ 10^K (orders 4·5^{K−1} and 2^{K−2}), hence K ≥ 2 log10 G.

**M3 (cost floor of any pattern-as-unit sieve; STRONG-EMPIRICAL plus an argument, not a lower bound over all
algorithms).** Work is at least the size of the surviving pattern list at the widest stage; level-12 survival is exactly
generic, 1/49.5 for every Tier-C vertex (index-40 subgroup times the 19.2% non-zero-divisor fraction; Monte Carlo
confirms to 0.2%); no exact count or join beats the pattern list because the test is multiplicative and the pattern sum
additive.

**M4 (accidents bound the useful depth from below).** Proposition 1.5 plus the §1.6 caveats: the Anchoring Lemma is false
as stated, accident counts at Tier-C q are unknown, and only the sandwich removes them. The extrapolation that N_L ρ_L
crosses 1 near L ≈ 128 for the k = 44 multiset is HEURISTIC (`more.py`) and rests on the model reported refuted at q = 14.

**M5 (depth-1 Lemma C kills by counting only up to q ≈ 20).** A case survives with probability 1/640000 and the verdict
depends on classes e < a + 9, so expected survivors per pattern are about 2^{q(a+9)/96}/640000, which exceeds 1 once
q(a+9)/96 ≥ 19.3. Depth 2 never kills, so closure past depth 1 is by bound only.

**B1 (single-ended chain is geometric by construction; PROVEN-ON-PAPER).** The form for gap j carries N_j, whose height is
S_j log 10 + O(1), so S_{j+1} ≤ (1 + λ)S_j and log10 G ≥ k log10(1 + λ): exponential in k whenever λ > 1; a
polynomial-in-k bound needs λ ≤ 1 + O(1/k).

**B2 (the constant is not the issue).** Closing the k = 44 sandwich at depth ≤ 3 needs log10 G′ ≤ 168, i.e. per-gap
multiplier λ ≤ 10^3.6 single-ended or 10^7.0 two-ended, against the documented 10^18.6; Matveev's C(4) = 1.6×10^13,
C(5) = 1.3×10^15 times P_0 ≈ 3.4 and T ≈ 10^3 give 10^{18–19}. "No near-term explicit Baker constant reaches 10^7" is
HEURISTIC; the arithmetic of the requirement is exact. [B3]'s sensitivity (10^−12 at k = 44) agrees. [HYP] H3 (citing
[LIT2]): sharper bounds "do not change the exponential shape".

**B3 (two-ended).** The exponent cannot drop below (k/2) log10 λ for a scheme in which an interior gap is bounded only by
the prefix or suffix on one side (PROVEN-ON-PAPER as a statement about the recursion). Yu's published explicit constants
are about 45 times worse per gap than Matveev's ([BAKER] §7).

**B4 (reduction relocates the exponent; counting PROVEN-ON-PAPER, pruning HEURISTIC).** Baker–Davenport reduction with
bound B gives gaps ≤ about 3 log10 B, converging in three rounds to n ≈ 4×10^2 for k = 44, but the number of sparse
prefixes is C(3 log10 B, k)·(multiset arrangements): 10^102 at log10 G = 850, 10^90.7 at 1400-scale, 10^70.7 at
log10 G = 168; with the residues mod 96 the counts are 10^19.0, 10^13.0, 10^6.3 before multiplication by the pattern
count.

**L1–L4 (the sandwich).** L1: D ≈ 0.4k. L2: the case count is at most (D + 1)^q, superexponential with D ~ 0.4k; a
measured live sub-coset growth of ×3–5 per depth for k ≤ 15 (FRONTIER.md, as cited in [B1]) is "unresolved" in [B1]; the chat
entry of 23:05 attributes that growth to the collapsed enumeration (the `(d, e)` dictionary defect reported in
[SANDWICH]) and expects (D + 1)^q-like growth after correction, consistent with Proposition 5.3; no B2-lane report was
read. L3: splitting pinned digits between the two ends costs more than pinning all on one side. L4: the exponent-lattice
rank (below).

**A1–A2 (anchor-first and per-anchor closure).** Anchor-first replaces 10^22 level-12 patterns by about 10^5 anchors per
vertex carrying 10^{11}–10^{14} level-96 patterns (matching the independent forecast within ×0.9–1.7), but every anchored
pattern is open at every level (M1), depth-1 Lemma C closes 90–99.99%, leaving 10^9–10^12 per vertex needing depth 7–18,
so cost ≥ (anchored patterns) × (depth-1 survival) × (D + 1)^q. [B1] A2 labels CONJECTURE that a per-anchor collapse
beyond depth 1 exists at all.

**S1 (Subspace theorem).** Evertse's inequality gives, for every ε > 0, n − E_{j+1} ≤ εn + C(ε) with C ineffective
([LIT2] §1.7); effective counts known are doubly exponential in k (Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt
exp((6N)^{3N}(r + 1)); Amoroso–Viada (8N)^{4N^4(N+r+1)}, N = k + 3, r = 4). S2: a polynomial-in-k length bound follows
from abc-strength input (about 5×10^3 digits at k = 44 under Robert–Stewart–Tenenbaum); conditional, and not admissible
under RULE 2.

**Literature shape ([LIT2]).** Every effective bound for S-units with few nonzero digits has the shape (C log n)^{k+O(1)}
(Stewart 1980; Bugeaud–Kaneko 2018; Bugeaud 2018 and 2026); no published effective bound is better than exponential in
k. Every complete resolution of a many-term exponential equation on record (Dimitrov–Howe 2021: 22–25 terms, ×3.5 per
extra term, 8.6 CPU-days at 22; Bertók–Hajdu: 9 unknowns; Bajpai–Bennett: 5 terms) is residue-class enumeration.

### 6.3 What the equations offer that the general theorems do not use

* **L4/P3, per-pattern exponent rank ([B1] §2.3, §3).** The pattern fixes y mod 3 and y mod 7 (§2). About 57% of patterns
  have 3 ∤ y and 7 ∤ y, so y = 2^a: finite once A(M) is known, no lattice, no Baker. About 38% have exactly one of b, c
  free: rank 1, the √det barrier becomes det and the bound per depth is about 10^96 rather than 10^48, with one
  logarithm fewer in the form (C(3) = 1.4×10^11 against C(4) = 1.6×10^13); (D + 1)^q falls from 10^56 to about 10^44 at
  k = 44. About 5% keep rank 2 at full cost, and a vertex is closed only when every branch is, so the worst case is not
  reduced. The orders are PROVEN-ON-PAPER arithmetic; that the coset representative is not unusually small is
  HEURISTIC (false for an anchored pattern, handled by the exact nearest-point computation). Not implemented.
* **P4a, forced 2-adic tails.** For v_2(y) = a the low terms satisfy 9Σ_{e_j<a} w_j 10^{e_j} ≡ 1 (mod 2^a); each next
  position is a function of the digits chosen so far, so the number of admissible tails of length l divisible by 2^l
  grows like l^5.2 (for 2^39 3^3 7^2: 1, 3, 5, 11, 19 for l = 1..5; 291, 7960, 63218, 286742, 924421, 2387368 for
  l = 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60) against 2^l = 1.15×10^18 at l = 60. The forcing statement is PROVEN-ON-PAPER (elementary
  2-adic arithmetic); the growth rate is STRONG-EMPIRICAL to l = 60 (the search hit a state cap at l = 63). It collapses
  the depth-1 copy choices for dominated multisets and replaces the bottom Yu chain below position a by an exact
  automaton; positions in [a + 9, 96D) remain 2-adically free, so depth ≥ 2 is not collapsed. The same search computes
  A(M) where its tree is finite, which the engine's `two_adic_bound` does not for k ≥ 36.
* **Not exploited and judged unproductive by [B1]:** the all-1 background (P1), the small coefficient set (P2, constant
  factors only), cluster decomposition of runs of adjacent digits (P6, worst case unchanged), 3-adic structure for
  large b (P4b, untested, CONJECTURE that it adds nothing).
* **Heuristic truth ([B1] §1.4, HEURISTIC).** About 4.1n² S-units have n digits; with the sparse-shape count the
  expected number of solutions for 2^39 3^3 7^2 (k = 44) peaks at 10^−31.6 at n = 49 and sums to 10^−234 over n ≥ 300; for
  2^23 3^7 7 (k = 31), 10^−18.7 at n = 34 and 10^−244 beyond 300. The proof gap is about log10 n: roughly 850 proven
  against about 2 expected.

A per-gap bound gap ≤ C·h^θ with θ ≤ 0.75 for this family would close Tier C at depth 1 (log10 S_k below the depth-1
lower bound 10^72); θ = 0 (height-free) closes below the 10^27 of the lattice. Every known theorem has θ = 1.
The scale note of the source: a step change needs θ < 1 for this family or Baker constants about 10^8 smaller; "neither

not carry the height of the lower part (g_j ≤ C(S) log n for interior gaps, or S_{j+1} ≤ (1 + 1/k)S_j + poly), or an
effective Evertse inequality with ε = 1/k for the group ⟨2,3,5,7⟩ and coefficients at most 72 (for which no technique in the
documents suffices; CONJECTURE that it is within reach). The nature of the requirement in W1 terms: log10 G′ ≤ 168 at
k = 44 for depth 3 (cost then 4^44 = 10^26.5 cases), log10 G′ ≤ 120 for depth 2 (3^44 = 10^21).

**Target 2: a sandwich step subexponential in q ([B1] W2; [CDA]).** Present cost per pattern at D = 18, q = 44 is
(D + 1)^q = 10^56. A step with cost exp(o(q)) or polynomial in q, for the present G, would remove the third factor of
§6.1. Proposition 5.3 shows that any method producing explicit lists of the compatible sub-cosets pays 2^r, and
Proposition 5.4 shows that enclosing them in one affine lattice discards the information; so a W2 step must be implicit
(Proposition 5.1) and must supply certificates (Proposition 5.2) outside the failure regime. The task [CDA] names:
"minimize exponent height over the symbolic Boolean family without enumerating its fibers". [B1] N4 asks for a 5-adic
(or 10-adic) analog of the 2-adic forcing (§6.3 P4a) that fixes copy choices without knowing the coset. Candidates
examined without success in [B1]: dual enumeration over the coset image (H7, H13: a crossover in scale, about 40× at
k = 29, not subexponential) and a meet in the middle against the multiplicative image (no mechanism found).

**Target 3 (accidents, N3).** A rigorous character-sum or large-sieve bound for sparse sums Σ w_j 10^{e_j} in cosets of
⟨2,3,7⟩ modulo the primes of 10^L − 1 would settle the accident count and Anchoring (H1/H10) as a theorem; it would
close nothing alone ([B1] P7).

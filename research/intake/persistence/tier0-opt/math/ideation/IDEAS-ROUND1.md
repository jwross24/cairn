# Ideation round 1: cheaper base-10 search for Tier B and Tier C

Lane: ideation (modes-of-reasoning pass, then idea-wizard winnowing). Scope: new mathematics or algorithms that remove
orders of magnitude from Tier B (13 vertices, 3e15 to 1.6e17 level-12 patterns, k = 20 to 29) and Tier C (6 vertices,
at least 1.7e19, k = 31 to 44). Nothing here edits HYPOTHESES.md, tier0/ or any existing file. Tags follow RULE 2:
CONJECTURE and STRONG-EMPIRICAL only; nothing is PROVEN. No step rests on abc or any unproven conjecture.

Entry format follows HYPOTHESES.md (claim; factor removed; kill test under 2 CPU-hours with a prediction registered
before running; owner; status), plus confidence and a "correlated with H1 to H4" line. Scripts that produced every
number below are in `scripts/` (run with `uv run --no-project --with sympy --with numpy python <file>`).

## Bottom line

No entry removes the six or more orders of magnitude Tier C needs. The best single entry removes about one order of
magnitude from every stage at once (R1.1, sound, cheap, measured). The rest are factors of 2 to 40 or planning facts.
The structural reason is in R1.2: the work is the two lift stages (12 to 24, then 24 to 48), whose tests outnumber their
survivors by 10^7 to 10^8 at Tier B and 10^8 or more at Tier C, and every output-sensitive mechanism examined here
(target-first decoding, joins against the multiplicative image) runs out of leverage because the target set is as large as
the box it replaces. What would remove orders of magnitude is a method that is output-sensitive at both lifts; none is
found here.

## Ranked entries

### R1.1 Branch split: choose the subgroup from the pattern's residues mod 3 and mod 7

- Claim. For a pattern with n = nu (mod 12), the integer y' satisfies y' = n + W (mod 3) and y' mod 7 is a function of
  (nu mod 6, class sums mod 6). So 3 divides y' exactly when nu + W = 0 (mod 3), and 7 divides y' exactly when the mod 7
  value is 0. If 3 does not divide y' then b = 0; if 7 does not divide y' then c = 0. The sieve tests membership in
  <2,3,7> for every pattern. It should test membership in <2,3,7> when both divide, <2,3> when only 3 divides, <2,7>
  when only 7 divides, and <2> otherwise. The test is sound (a true child is a member of the subgroup that matches its
  own valuations) and strictly stronger.
- Factor removed. Random patterns fall in the four classes with probabilities 1/21, 2/7, 2/21, 4/7, and the rank-3 test
  keeps every class at rate 1/I(2,3,7). With the class-matched subgroup the survival rate is
  (1/21)/I(2,3,7) + (2/7)/I(2,3) + (2/21)/I(2,7) + (4/7)/I(2). Exact from F1: survivors drop by 12.59 at L = 12, 20.91 at
  L = 24, 21.00 at L = 48 and L = 96. The parents fed to every lift drop by 12.6 and the survivors of the later levels by
  21, so the whole pipeline (every tier, every stage after level 12, and the accident floor of F4) shrinks by 12 to 21.
  The engine excludes the primes 3 and 7 and never uses the 21 part of R_96 = 21 Q, which the H1 lane also notes is
  untested for anchors; the branch split is the sieve use of that part.
- Kill test (about 10 CPU-minutes, run for the sampling half; the engine half is registered): `branchtest.py` samples 4e6
  uniform level-12 patterns for each of three random multisets (k = 12 and 14). Rank-3 survivors 80.6k to 81.2k (2.0
  percent, the 1/49.5 that dpcount reports), branch-split survivors 6.3k to 6.6k, ratio 12.76, 12.38, 12.58 (prediction 12.6
  from F1). `planted.py`: all 8,180 genuine zero-free S-units with at least 7 digits (a < 140, b < 90, c < 50, all four
  classes present) pass the branch-split test; forcing the wrong subgroup (<2> for everything) passes only the 20 units
  with b = c = 0, so the gate refuses. Registered, not yet run: in the Python reference engine, vertex 8192 (M = 2^13,
  N12 = 2.995e7) and vertex 28672 (N12 = 1.947e8) at levels 12, 24, 48, 96 with the class-matched test. Prediction:
  level-12 survivors fall by 12.6 +/- 1.0; every recorded child is still recovered (no POSITIVE CONTROL LOST); the final
  OPEN sets and anchors are identical; level-24 children fall by at least 12. Falsifier: a known child lost, or a
  reduction below 8.
- Confidence HIGH. Soundness is elementary divisibility; the factor is exact index arithmetic and was reproduced by
  sampling at L = 12; the planted-positive and planted-negative both behave. Seam: the factor at L = 24 and above is
  index arithmetic, not sampled (the discrete logarithms there need the engine's tables).
- Correlated with H1 to H4: touches H1 only through the untested 21 part (H1 lane, A1). Independent of H2, H3, H4.
  Composes multiplicatively with every other entry.

### R1.2 Allocate effort by the stage-ratio law (the wall is the lift, then the second lift)

- Claim. At k = 25 the 12 to 24 lift performs about 224 tests per level-12 pattern and the 24 to 48 lift about 20, so the
  level-12 pass is under 1 percent of Tier B work; the 24 to 48 lift equals the first lift near k = 31 and exceeds it
  beyond, so for the four Tier C vertices with k = 36 to 44 the second lift is the wall. FRONTIER.md prices Tier B as the
  level-12 pass on a 10^10 per second GPU kernel; that prices the stage that is under 1 percent of the work. Speedups
  must be judged against the stage that dominates at the k of the vertex.
- Factor removed. None directly. It prevents spending a two-order-of-magnitude engineering effort (a level-12 GPU
  kernel) on 0.4 percent of Tier B cost, and it ranks the research targets: output-sensitive 12 to 24 lift for Tier B, an
  output-sensitive 24 to 48 lift for Tier C. The gap between tests and outputs is 10^7 to 10^8 at Tier B (3.7e16 to 1.2e17 tests
  against 2e9 outputs for the smallest vertex) and larger at Tier C.
- Kill test (about 1 CPU-hour): run the reference or C pipeline on three equations with k = 16 or 17 and N12 at most 3e9 and
  count lift tests. Registered prediction: 12 to 24 tests per level-12 pattern 0.87 at k = 16 and 1.6 at k = 17 (within a
  factor 2); 24 to 48 tests per pattern 5.8e-3 and 1.4e-2 (within a factor 3); open-after-tail fraction in [3e-4, 8e-4].
  Killed if either lift is off by more than the stated factor, because the Tier B and C figures are extrapolated from k at most 14.
- Confidence MEDIUM. The ratios are measured for k = 8 to 14 over thousands of equations and extrapolated; the growth
  rates could bend. Direction (lifts dominate) is robust: the exponent per digit exceeds the level-12 exponent.
- Correlated with H1 to H4: none. It is a measurement; H4 (BCJ) and H2 target different stages.

### R1.3 Target-first (dual) enumeration of the level-24 survivors, pooled across vertices

- Claim. A level-L pattern value is the integer U = R_nu + sum of c_r 10^r (class sums c_r, sum W), at most (W+0.12) * 10^(L-1),
  and the sieve is a function of U mod Q_L only. For L = 24 the residues that can survive are the image of
  (a, b, c) -> 2^a 3^b 7^c, which has |<3,7>_24| * (A+1) = 3.37e13 (A+1) elements. For each such residue h the candidates are
  the integers U = h + m Q_24 with m up to 18.9 W, where Q_24 = 11*13*37*101*9901*73*137*99990001 = 5.29e21. A candidate
  stays only if its decimal digit sum is at most W and is congruent to W mod 9; the survivors are decoded into
  class-sum vectors by uncarrying and then realized against each multiset. The target set depends on neither the
  vertex nor the multiset, so one pass serves every vertex with W at most 126.
- Factor removed. The first lift (tests = parents times children) is replaced by about 3.37e13 (A+1) * 18.9 W steps. For
  Tier B with A + 1 = 36 and W = 70 that is 1.6e18 steps against 3.7e16 lift tests at k = 20 (the dual loses by 40) and
  8e19 at k = 29 (the dual wins by 40). For the smallest Tier C vertex (A + 1 = 130, W = 85) it is 7e18 steps against 2.6e22 to
  1.5e23 tests: a factor 4e3 to 2e4 on that stage. Break-even is near k = 24 to 25. The 24 to 48 lift is untouched, and F3
  puts it at 10 to 40 percent of the first lift for Tier B, about equal near k = 31, and dominant above, so the whole-
  pipeline gain is at most about 2.5 to 10 at Tier B and at most about 1.5 at the k = 31 vertex. R1.1 composes: requiring 21
  to divide U cuts the m range by 21 for the generic class.
- Kill test (about 20 CPU-minutes in C, or 2 hours in numpy): logic check at level 12 where the dual is cheap. Vertex 8192
  (W = 13): T = 8.91e6 (A+1) targets, m up to 246, about 3e10 steps. Prediction: the set of integers U that the dual
  returns equals exactly the set of U values of the primal sweep over all 2.995e7 patterns that pass the residue test, and
  the realization count per U matches the engine's `(a,pattern)` statistic. The cost model above is then checked by
  timing steps per second. Killed if the sets differ (a flaw in the decoding) or if the measured step rate times the model
  puts break-even above k = 30.
- Confidence MEDIUM-LOW. The decoding is exact arithmetic; the economics rest on A (the true 2-adic bound, unknown above
  k = 35), on the tail test not being usable pooled (a per-digit-set run recovers a factor 10 to 160 for Tier B but loses
  pooling), and on a second lift the dual does not touch.
- Correlated with H1 to H4: related to H4 and to the codex brainstorm item 3 (join against the multiplicative image) as the
  limiting case where the whole image is enumerated; it does not use representations. Independent of H1 to H3.

### R1.4 Tail-word conditioning turns the multiplicative target into small explicit sets, then a join

- Claim. IDEAS.md section B and the H4 lane reject meet-in-the-middle because the target is a subgroup of density
  1/40 and a sum of two halves cannot be tested for membership. Conditioning on the lowest K decimal digits of y' removes
  the objection: the pair (a, tail word w) determines (b, c) modulo a 10-adic lattice, and intersected with the residue class it
  leaves a small explicit set Z_w of admissible residues for the remaining digits (estimate 1e3 to 1e5 at K = 5, level 12; unmeasured at level 24). Within each bucket a two-list join costs
  about sqrt(N_b) * |Z_w| against N_b for a plain scan.
- Factor removed. The join gain is sqrt(N * #w) / |T|, with N the patterns, #w the number of tail-word buckets and |T| the
  total targets. Assuming, for the smallest Tier B vertex at level 24, N = 2e20, #w = 1e8 and |T| = 2e10 (the last from F2 survivors divided by the expected 0.05 solutions per target), it is 2.5; to
  reach 100 the tail words would need 10^4 times more buckets than the pinned K supports. Applies to both lifts.
- Kill test (about 40 CPU-minutes): histogram |Z_w| at level 12 for vertex 8192 using the engine's `K*` routine over
  every (a, residue class). Prediction: the median |Z_w| over occupied words lies in [3e3, 1e5] at K = 5, and the model
  gain for a k = 25 Tier B equation at K* = 10 (level 24) lies in [2, 10]. Killed if the modeled gain is below 2.
- Confidence LOW. The upper end of the range depends on tail words longer than the coset pins; the lower end is a
  constant-factor win.
- Correlated with H1 to H4: extends H4 (answers its stated density obstruction) and matches codex brainstorm item 3. Counts
  once with H4.

### R1.5 One integer sweep for the level-12 stage of every vertex

- Claim. The level-12 test depends only on the integer U = R_nu + sum c_r 10^r mod 5,291,005,291 and on the vertex's A. U lies
  below 1.3e13, whatever the number of digits. All 177 vertices can share one sweep of at most 1.3e13 integers (about 36
  CPU-hours at 10 ns) that records which U pass for each A, with the realizations against each multiset (uncarry plus a
  knapsack over the multiset) done only for survivors.
- Factor removed. Stage one only: Tier B plus C is 2.9e22 patterns today (sum of N12 in FRONTIER.md). By F3 that stage is
  under 1 percent of the work for k at least 25, so the whole-pipeline effect is about 1 percent at Tier B and nothing at
  Tier C. It matters as the shared front end of R1.3 and as the cheap exact source of the survivor lists that R1.1 and
  R1.3 filter. Per vertex the class-sum count is below the pattern count only for the large-k vertices (24 to 7e4 for Tier
  C, 0.6 to 160 for Tier B, below 1 for Tier A), so the vertex-independent form is the one that pays.
- Kill test (about 15 CPU-minutes): for vertex 8192 and for one Tier A equation with recorded `c_stats`, regenerate the
  level-12 survivor count from the integer sweep plus realization and compare with the recorded zero,
  prefilter-dead and (a,pattern) counts. Prediction: exact agreement; the integer sweep visits at most 1.3e13 values
  regardless of the vertex.
- Confidence HIGH for correctness, LOW for impact. Exact reformulation of a stage already cheap at high k.
- Correlated with H1 to H4: none (dpcount, H2). Overlaps the "N = sum c_r 10^r" pointer in RESULT.md's Tier 1 sizing.

### R1.6 Accident constant as a measured quantity, and the completeness obligation it creates for H1 and H2

- Claim. The non-anchored survivors at levels 24 and 48 follow model(L, M) times a constant f = 1.2e-3 (F2), so the
  accident count at level 96 for Tier B and Tier C can be forecast rather than argued about (F4). Tier B: at most 2e-5
  per vertex at the floor (2e-2 at f = 1), so an anchored census is complete in effect. Tier C: 2.3 to 1.8e8 per vertex at the
  floor, up to 1.5e11 at f = 1, so every Tier C vertex has non-anchored open patterns that no anchor-first or per-anchor closure will see, and they
  need either a level beyond 96 or the Baker sandwich with their own coset.
- Factor removed. None. It prices the completeness obligation: closing the anchored 99.7 percent or more is not closing
  the vertex, and the remainder cannot be enumerated by the anchor method.
- Kill test (the first equation with k = 15 to 19 and three or more recorded levels, no new compute): Registered prediction:
  f24 in [0.8e-3, 2e-3], and the non-anchored deaths at level 48 per equation Poisson with mean f * model(48). Killed if f24 drifts
  by a factor above 3 across k = 14 to 18 (then Tier C accidents scale otherwise).
- Confidence MEDIUM. Model calibrated over k = 11 to 14 and consistent with the H1 lane's decisive run; extrapolation to
  k = 31 to 44 is the open seam (A and the tail test change).
- Correlated with H1 to H4: restates H1's accident analysis with a calibrated constant; counts with H1.

## Examined and dropped on paper (do not repeat without the stated reason)

- Cyclic rotation of positions as a symmetry of the test (a factor L): needs 10 in <2,3,7>; F1 shows I(2,3,5,7) = 10 against
  I(2,3,7) = 40 at L = 12 (5,760 against 138,240 at L = 24).
- Carrying a as a lattice coordinate (rank-3 coset) through the lifts: tier0v2.py keeps survivors per (a, nu, eps, coset)
  and the tail test needs a, so it removes most (a, pattern) pairs before any lift; reasoning from the code, not measured.
- Testing level 24 directly with no level-12 filter: N24 is 2e20 for the smallest Tier B vertex against 3.7e16 to 1.2e17
  staged lift tests, so the staged order is the cheaper one.
- A 10-adic cut at levels 12 or 24 with long K: its power is limited by the 2- and 5-primary content of the coset image
  (Lemma C at 96 gets 4*5^4 and 2^8); expected to overlap what I(2,3,7) already removes. Not measured.
- 3-adic or 7-adic zero-target sieves for candidates with huge b or c: n is a free integer, so a congruence mod 3^j
  reduces to the digit sum conditions mod 3 and 9 (the mod 3 one is R1.1; mod 9 needs the level to be a multiple of 36).
- A persistence-depth shortcut that avoids classifying Tier C children: any child y of a frontier vertex gives an N with
  p(N) = y and persistence one higher, so the full child classification is equivalent to the persistence bound.
- Level-12-only accelerations (GPU kernel, pooled class sums): see R1.2 and R1.5; under 1 percent of Tier B work at k of 25 or more.

## Ranking rationale

R1.1 first: sound, exact factor 12.6 to 21 on every stage, measured, cheap to implement in the existing C level-12 pass and
the Python coset class. R1.2 second: it redirects effort and costs one CPU-hour. R1.3 and R1.4 follow: they attack the
dominant stage but cap near an order of magnitude whole-pipeline. R1.5 and R1.6 are exact but small or planning-only.

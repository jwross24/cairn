# new_primes(768, 384): Pohlig-Hellman feasibility for k >= 31

Status: computation notes; the factorizations below are exact, the bound estimates are labelled. Not a result on any vertex.
Question (chat 17:05): can the level-768 prime cut (needed for k >= 31, where U'' exceeds the level-384 bound 10^239) be run with discrete logs?

## The primes

`new_primes(L, L')` = prime factors of Phi_d(10), d | L, d not dividing L'. For (768, 384) the divisors are d = 256 and d = 768:
Phi_256(10) = 10^128 + 1 (129 digits), Phi_768(10) = 10^256 - 10^128 + 1 (256 digits). Factorization commands: `tools/ph768_b.gp` (PARI/GP 2.17,
`factor(N, 10^9)`, trial division to 10^9, found 257, 15361, 453377 in Phi_256(10) and 434689, 859393 in Phi_768(10); script `tools/ph768_a.gp`), checked and extended
with `tools/ph768.py` (sympy `factorint`, `isprime`, `n_order`).

| factor | digits | ord_p(10) | p - 1 | largest prime power of p - 1 | PH group operations (sum e * 2 sqrt(q)) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 257 | 3 | 256 | 2^8 | 256 | 16 |
| 15361 | 5 | 256 | 2^10 * 3 * 5 | 1024 | 26 |
| 453377 | 6 | 256 | 2^8 * 7 * 11 * 23 | 256 | 34 |
| 434689 | 6 | 768 | 2^9 * 3 * 283 | 512 | 52 |
| 859393 | 6 | 768 | 2^8 * 3^2 * 373 | 373 | 58 |
| cofactor of Phi_256(10) | 116 | | probable prime (`isprime`), p - 1 not factored | | outside the 40-digit cap of `new_primes` |
| cofactor of Phi_768(10) | 245 | | composite, no further factor known to this run (see below) | | outside the cap |

`new_primes(768, 384)` with the Python cap (`cap_digits = 40`, `ph_cap = 10^8`) returns exactly the five primes 257, 15361, 434689, 453377, 859393.
All five have p - 1 smooth with largest prime 283 (373 for 859393), so a discrete log costs under 60 group operations (BSGS tables of size <= 20) and
the largest prime power in any p - 1 is 1024. There is no PH obstruction at level 768: the cost is negligible next to the 10-adic cut.
Level 384 for comparison: the largest prime factor of p - 1 over {3457, 12289, 418725889, 1265011073} is 1217 (p = 418725889); level 192: 463949 (p = 1253224535459902849).

The cofactors are not usable: the 116-digit factor is prime with unfactored p - 1; the 245-digit one is composite and unfactored
(`tools/ph768_ecm.py`: sympy `ecm`, B1 = 2e4, B2 = 1e6, 60 curves, returned no factor in 25 s; weak evidence only, not a proof of absence). Any prime subset is a valid weaker level, so omitting them costs index, not soundness.
Primes between 10^7 and 10^40 inside the cofactors cannot be excluded by this run.

## What the five primes buy (estimate, `tools/ph768_lattice.py`, `logs/ph768_lattice.txt`)

Exact lattice indices (Python big integers, kernel lattices of the logs of 3 and 7):

| lattice | index | shortest basis vector |
|---|---|---|
| Lambda_96 | 10^56.45 | 10^27.89 |
| Lambda_192 | 10^91.90 | 10^45.87 |
| Lambda_384 | 10^103.63 | 10^51.41 |
| Lambda_768 (384 primes + the five above) | 10^110.02 | 10^54.55 |

The five primes add 10^6.4 of index (the images of <3, 7> there are small subgroups). The gain at level 768 comes from the 10-adic window K = 768, not from the primes.
Homogeneous window kernel (coset of the origin for a = 0, 1; shortest vector of the kernel lattice, a proxy for the nearest-point distance of a typical coset):

| level | a | kernel index | shortest vector | digit bound for a typical coset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 384 | 0 | 10^480.22 | 10^240.04 | 10^239.6 |
| 384 | 1 | 10^479.92 | 10^239.90 | 10^239.4 |
| 768 | 0 | 10^870.61 | 10^435.00 | 10^434.5 |
| 768 | 1 | 10^870.31 | 10^435.00 | 10^434.5 |

Check against measurement: at level 384 the exact bounds on the 1,335 patterns are 10^238.1 to 10^239.4 (RESULT.md), within two orders of the kernel proxy
(10^239.4 to 10^239.6). If level 768 behaves alike, the bounds are about 10^432 to 10^434, against U'' = 10^246.02 (k = 31, a = 0) and 10^344.98 (k = 44, a = 0):
a margin of more than 85 orders at k = 44. This is an extrapolation from the kernel; no level-768 coset has been computed for a pattern.

## Not covered

- The shadow-certificate structure at level 768 (trivial images, the difference formula for the 384 -> 768 step) is not examined. At the five primes 10^384 == -1
  (order 768: 10^384 is the element of order 2; order 256: 10^384 = 10^128 == -1), so the 384 -> 768 branch values have the same form as 192 -> 384, V - 2 Delta.
- Cost of the 768 window cut and of the DP/MITM at k >= 31 was not measured.
- No k >= 31 pattern exists in the data; k = 31..44 is the Tier-C range.

Verdict: discrete logs at level 768 are feasible (five primes, under 60 group operations each); the primes contribute 10^6.4 of index, the window contributes the bound.

## Part 1 — proposals

### 1 · Nothing gates what leaves the harness · HIGH · §4 gate layer, §10, §11

Every gate here is an *admission* gate: it decides what enters the substrate and what tag a node carries. Nothing
decides what the harness **says**. The one documented fabrication in the plan's own evidence base is an output
artifact, not a node — AI Scientist v1 "hallucinated an ablations table" (`PROPOSALS.md` C5, arXiv 2408.06292v3 §8),
in a written paper. Cairn would catch a fabricated substrate node and never look at the report. §11 calls the
negative-results map "citable", §10 calls state "legible"; neither is a checked surface, and "submission" appears
in §4 and §12 undefined.

**Adoption bar.** Component: gate layer (§4), one new admission gate. Cost: a gate-owned renderer plus a reference
check (~150 lines); failure surface is a blocked report when the claims DB lags, which is the correct direction.
Evidence: STRONG-EMPIRICAL in the surveyed systems (`PROPOSALS.md` C5 incident table). It dissolves no gate and lets
strategy edit nothing — the tag it prints is the derived one.

```diff
 - **Gate layer (mechanical, immutable):** submission verifier (`xP==Q` subprocess or no
   submit, ever) · small-scale ladder §6 · no-go checklist §8 · formalization gate §7 ·
   reproducibility gate (no substrate node = inadmissible; §3 re-run) · proportional-scrutiny
-  router §7 · tier gate §5.
+  router §7 · tier gate §5 · egress gate (below).
+  - *Egress gate.* Nothing leaves the harness except through a rendered report. An **egress
+    artifact** — an operator report, a draft, a negative-results entry (§11), a submission —
+    is generated by a gate-owned renderer from claim statement nodes: every assertion in it
+    names a claim statement hash and is printed with that version's derived tag (§7) and its
+    evidence pointer, and free prose asserting anything that names no statement hash is
+    refused, not stripped. A claim at `refuted` or `withdrawn` cannot appear as an assertion.
+    Submission is the egress artifact whose assertion is a recovered `x`; it additionally
+    requires the verifier's passing node, which is what "no submit, ever" names. The renderer
+    and the refusal predicate are gate-bundle fields; the gate ships the §4 planted-failure
+    pair (an artifact asserting an unregistered claim must FAIL; a fully-referenced one must
+    PASS). *Why:* the documented fabrications in the surveyed systems were in reports, and a
+    harness that gates only its own storage has left its output surface ungated.
```

### 4 · The verification escrow's release rule contradicts its spend rule in the normal case · MED · §5

## (a) The three I would stake the most on

**1** (egress gate), **2** (define the human path), **3** (shrink M0 to a slice). 1 and 2 are missing gates on the
two surfaces where this design's own evidence base says systems actually fail — the written report and the
human-attested verdict. 3 is the plan enforcing its own closing rule on itself.

## (b) Where the framing is wrong

The plan is now a specification of an epistemic accounting system with a research harness attached. §4 gives the
Librarian, Reframer, Experimentalist and Prover one clause each; nothing states what a research move *is*, how a
subproblem becomes a hypothesis object, or what the Reframer does with a blocked branch. Every mechanism added
since r0 is a refusal mechanism. The baseline says the realistic target is publishable increments — and no
milestone's done-when is an increment the harness produced that nobody planted. M3's "motion half" bar is the right
instinct, but its motion carries a *known* advance (negation-map rho) to KEEP, and a fixture cannot demonstrate
discovery. Add to M4: one negative-results entry or one library lemma, produced end-to-end on a subproblem no
fixture seeded, admitted at STRONG-EMPIRICAL or PROVEN. If M4 clears every other bar and cannot produce that, the
harness is an excellent referee and not a researcher, and the operator should learn it from a milestone rather than
from a year of green gates.

The second risk follows: §2 and §4 now carry single bullets running eighty lines with nested em-dash clauses, and
they fail the readability standard the plan imposes on everyone else. A fresh implementer cannot build `justify`'s
coverage check from §7's prose — and `justify` is an M0 deliverable whose scope type decides every tag in the system.

## (c) Facts I would need that the plan does not state

1. The **type** of the claim statement node's `scope` and the evidence node's `declared population` — the superset
   and subset checks in `justify` are M0 done-when items and cannot be written from "the same typed family, size and
   range fields". A closed grammar (family enum · size interval · assumption set) is needed before M0 starts.
2. Whether the operator and the orchestrator run as **two OS users** on the build machine, which the pin, the
   checkpoint and proposal 2 all require. §13 grounds the append-only flag but never lists the two-user deployment
   as an M0 deliverable.
3. What "**submission**" means operationally — to whom, through what channel, with what human act. Proposal 1
   assumes it is an egress artifact; the plan never says.
4. The distinction between an `informs` edge and a `justified_by` edge (§7 relies on it; only the latter is typed).
5. Expected **human review throughput** — items per week the operator will actually service — without which
   proposal 5's alarm has no threshold and M4's parallel tracks have no sizing.
